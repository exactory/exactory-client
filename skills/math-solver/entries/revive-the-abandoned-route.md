## Trigger

- The journal or the literature records a route abandoned for one specific obstruction (a factor lost at one step, a hypothesis not preserved, a stall at a documented value), and not because of a proof that the route cannot work.
- A later move has changed the framework (a new measure of size, a new class of structured pieces, a new engine) in a way that removes exactly that obstruction.

## Action

1. Re-read the abandoned route and state its obstruction in one sentence.
2. Check that the new framework removes it: does the lost factor vanish, is the hypothesis now preserved under the passage that destroyed it?
3. Re-run the abandoned route inside the new framework. Prefer it over the framework's own newer route when it is cleaner.
4. Compare the bound with the newer route's bound and record which step now dominates.

## Output form

A proof along the old route inside the new framework.

## Failure signal

The obstruction reappears in a new guise (the same factor lost at a different step); or the new framework's advantage is exactly what the old route cannot use.

## Typical cash-out

Quantitative improvement; survey or exposition, when the revived route explains the newer one.
