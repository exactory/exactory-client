## Trigger

- The statement is extremal: a maximum or a minimum over a class of objects, or an exponent or constant that the truth attains.
- No candidate extremal object has been fixed, or the candidate has not been tested against every construction principle the ambient permits.

## Action

1. Build candidates from each construction principle: the most symmetric object, products of small optimal examples, algebraic constructions (a lattice, a strictly convex surface projected to the integers, a block construction), the random object.
2. Compute the value of the target quantity for each candidate. The best gives the conjectured constant or exponent and a lower bound.
3. Test the candidate for rigidity: is it unique up to the ambient symmetry? If several unrelated constructions tie, no argument can proceed by showing that the extremal object is rigid.
4. State the conjecture with the exact constant and the candidate, and record the gap to the best known bound in the other direction.

## Output form

A lower-bound construction with its value, a conjectured extremal constant, and a rigidity assessment.

## Failure signal

Constructions of different types all give the same value with no common structure (a sign of non-rigidity and a warning against rigidity-based proofs); or no construction beats the trivial one; or the candidate's value cannot be computed exactly.

## Typical cash-out

Quantitative improvement (a lower bound); computational evidence; problem paper.
