## Trigger

- A model or object realising the target has been constructed, in the attack or in the literature, and the construction fixed a side quantity as a by-product: the size of the continuum or of another cardinal invariant, the truth of a companion hypothesis that the construction destroyed or forced, a chain condition.
- The problem list, or a neighbouring result, asks whether the target is compatible with other values of the side quantity, or with the companion hypothesis the construction destroyed.

## Action

1. Name the side effect and locate the step of the construction that fixes it: the support of an iteration, a preliminary collapse, the kind of object adjoined.
2. Stack a second construction on top of the first that changes the side quantity (adjoin many objects of a different kind), and check each lemma the target's proof used against the new stage: which lemmas persist and which the new objects break.
3. Where a lemma breaks, replace the offending step by a variant that preserves the side condition (a different forcing with the same effect on the target, a support that avoids the object that broke the lemma) and reprove the target with the variant.
4. Record the range of the side quantity, and the companion hypotheses, with which the target is now known to be compatible.

## Output form

The target together with each value of the side quantity, or jointly with the companion hypothesis: a family of models, stated as a joint consistency or a compatibility theorem.

## Failure signal

Every variant that changes the side quantity breaks the target at the same lemma; that lemma is then a candidate theorem that the target forces the side quantity, to be proved by axiomatise-the-method-and-build-a-near-miss.

## Typical cash-out

Conditional or special-case result (joint consistency); quantitative improvement (the range of the side quantity).
