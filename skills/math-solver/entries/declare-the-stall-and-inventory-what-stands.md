## Trigger

- The journal shows the number of consecutive moves set by the skill whose failure signal fired, or a component stuck against a proved ceiling with no unexplored entry whose trigger matches the problem shape.
- No new problem-shape feature has been recorded during the last pass.
- Or the attack has reached its cash-out by any of the skill's three rules: a stall is due, no composition remains, or the attack has closed.

## Action

1. Stop attacking. List every statement proved during the attack: conditionals with their hypothesis, special cases with their subclass, explicit bounds with their constants, reductions and equivalences, barriers with their admissible class, counterexamples to neighbouring statements, computations with their range, lemmas whose hypotheses are generic.
2. For each statement, run the self-check: state it in one sentence; confirm that it is a claim and not a description of activity; confirm that it does not follow from known literature in a few lines; search for a prior proof; place it on or off the path to the target.
3. Group the statements that fit one narrative; separate those that do not into their own units.
4. Record the exact state of the attack (which entries fired, which failed, what the ceiling is) as the content of a problem paper.
5. Hand each unit to the cash-out procedure.

## Output form

A list of publishable units, each with its form label and its evidence source.

## Failure signal

No statement survives the self-check, because every candidate is known or trivial; then the output is a problem paper or a discussion post, not a paper.

## Typical cash-out

Every publication form, according to the unit; survey or problem paper for the residue.
