# Exactory 0.69.12

This patch states one rule for where a URL or a DOI ends in a bibliography, so that braces, quotes and backslashes
inside an identifier no longer let nested text cite a work. It also reads BibTeX escapes in DOIs.

## Why

The independent review of 0.69.11 found that URLs and DOIs still ended at `{`, `}`, `"` and `\`. With an unrelated
title, `doi={10.9999/x{arxiv:2609.14520}}` therefore cited `arxiv:2609.14520v1`. DOI syntax allows these characters,
and in BibTeX the inner braces group text and do not separate it.

## What changed

- A URL or a DOI ends at whitespace. It also ends before the closer of a bracket or a quote that directly precedes it
  (`>` for `<`, `](` for `[`, `"` for `"`), and before a `}` that has no matching `{` inside it, such as the brace that
  closes the BibTeX field.
- Every other character belongs to the identifier, including balanced braces, quotes, and a backslash together with
  the character after it.
- When a DOI is read, a backslash before a character other than a letter or a digit is dropped, so the BibTeX form
  `10.1002/abc\_def` reads as `10.1002/abc_def`.
- The entry reader does not count a brace after a backslash, so an escaped brace does not run an entry into the next
  field.
- Trailing punctuation, labels, `eprint` fields, old-style ids, the identifier sets, title matching and
  `find_citation_tokens` do not change.

## Upgrade

- A label is credited only when whitespace or one of the delimiters above separates it from every URL and DOI.
- A DOI written with BibTeX escapes, such as `\_`, now cites its work by identifier.
- The notes on BibTeX keys (since 0.69.8), on DOIs in publisher URLs (since 0.69.10), on Markdown link texts (since
  0.69.11) and on `already_cited` items (since 0.69.5) still apply. Publication bundles that are already recorded do not
  change.

## What stays the same

The problem citation projection, the reviewer prompts, the route-pinned transport and cost modules and
`review_state_evaluation.py` are unchanged.

## Checks

The new tests ran first against 0.69.11 and failed as expected (122 failed subtests). After the change, with
`PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 PYTHONPATH=tests`, the modules `test_research_citation_accounting`,
`test_research_problem_citations`, `test_research_providers`, `test_research_publication`,
`test_research_publication_records`, `test_research_publication_files`, `test_research_problem_delivery`,
`test_math_problems_cli`, `test_codex`, `test_manifest` and `test_research_guidance` (416 tests) passed on Python 3.12.8
and 3.9.6, and every performance case took less than one second. No existing expectation changed.
`codex/generate.py --check` and `git diff --check` pass.

## Corrections in 0.69.13

The independent review of this release found that it read an entry as one string. A quote inside braces closed a
quoted value, the title could come from inside another field, a URL inside a quoted field ran into the closing quote,
and percent-decoding before the removal of TeX escapes let `%5C.` read as `.`. The statement that nested text no
longer cites a work therefore did not hold in these cases. Release 0.69.13 reads entries by their top-level fields and
corrects all four.
