# Exactory 0.44.0

This release adds explicit operations for a study whose sources are partly
unavailable, and for a manuscript that publishes the supported part of an
objective that stays open. A scoped manuscript reaches independent reviewers
through a checked scientific delivery. The changes were first made and reviewed
as local repairs while the `2402.14112` study ran on them. The research
constitution stays at version 5. Every new operation and field is optional, so
a study that does not use them keeps its current path.

## Unavailable and embedded sources

- A supplement that is a section of the main original, not a separate file,
  counts as read when the required text units together cover the complete
  extracted main original. Each supplement still needs its acquisition
  provenance and its own inspection.
- `fulltext` takes `extraction_options: {"ocr": true}` for a scanned PDF. The
  harness runs pdfinfo, pdftoppm and Tesseract (English) with a 600-second
  deadline, at most 200 pages, a 5,000-pixel page dimension and 32 MiB for each
  rendered page and for the total text. It keeps the original PDF, every page
  boundary and the tool versions. OCR and `layout` are mutually exclusive. The
  tools are external, and their recorded provenance does not certify the text:
  scientific interpretation still needs the original page images.
- `fulltext` takes an optional `component` object. It acquires a supplement
  through an assessed `component_correspondence` with an already acquired main
  original. The assessment is a pinned JSON artifact that locates the
  requirement, the cover identity, the corresponding figures or sections and
  the compared conditions. It does not establish historical arXiv attachment
  identity or main-article equivalence. The new error codes are
  `invalid_component`, `component_parent_mismatch`, `component_hash_mismatch`,
  `component_requirement_missing`, `component_evidence_uninspected`,
  `component_pending` and `component_page_missing`.
- `defer-source` records an audited decision to continue without one
  unavailable source. It needs an authorization artifact and the retained
  retrieval evidence. Available material that still needs inventory or
  inspection cannot be deferred, and roots and verification targets cannot be
  deferred. The source-access and reading obligations of that version move to
  `deferred_obligations`, and the inventory still reports `fulltext_read:
  false`. A deferred version supplies no source evidence to a cycle, an
  assessment or a manuscript claim. A change to the bound captures, units,
  readings or requirements makes the decision `stale`. `resume-source` restores
  the ordinary obligations. `status --summary` counts active, stale, superseded
  and resumed decisions. Reviewers receive the source-gap disclosure without
  the private authorization or retrieval artifacts. The lineage gate does not
  require a citation for an actively deferred `classic` source; `lineage`
  sources stay required.

## Refreshing inherited assessments

`assess` takes an optional `inheritance_refresh: {plan_digest, bindings}`. A
reassessment of the same descendant execution can bind the current
reassessment of each ancestor explicitly, without a new plan or run. Each
binding keeps the original edge's evidence and all its assumptions. A binding
cannot raise a failure to a validated result. The plan, admissions, executions,
outputs, checkpoints and resource counters are unchanged; failure observations
are appended under the new assessment. Historical evaluation of a stale
ancestry now reports it as nonvalidated and incomplete, and still reports
artifact corruption, invalid evidence and forged lineage as errors.

## Source-limited manuscripts

- `publication-scope` records an immutable `source-limited-v1` contract on an
  assessed checkpoint: the scope, the supported claims with their evidence and
  assumptions, the public limitations, the objective obligations that stay
  deferred, the scientific preparers and an authorization artifact.
  `select-publication-scope` selects a contract or `null`, and `scoped-review`
  records an independent review of it.
- `gate manuscript-readiness` checks the selected contract, every ordinary
  evidence and development prerequisite, the coverage of the active deferrals
  and an independent scoped acceptance. `gate readiness` still checks the
  complete objective, which stays open. For a scoped study, finishing
  `experiment` and entering `write` need `manuscript-readiness` instead of
  `readiness`.
- An adverse scoped review blocks its scientific target for good. A different
  target needs a correction response to every adverse review and a new
  independent corrective review. A preparer of the study or of an earlier scope
  cannot review it.
- The manuscript pin compares the claim registry with the contract: id, text,
  polarity, assumptions, limitations and evidence of every approved claim.
  Continuity markers are round metadata: a `revised` marker does not take part
  in the comparison, and a `superseded` claim is not an approved claim. A
  `superseded` marker on a claim that the scope still supports is a mismatch.
  Blind reviewers receive the current claims without the markers.
- `exactory-draft deposit` and `exactory submit` take `--managed-only`, and
  `--scope-contract-id` with `--scope-target-digest`. The two scope arguments
  need each other and `--managed-only`. In this mode a refused check stops the
  command before any remote write. Without the flags, the commands keep the
  established direct path of 0.40.0.
- Each remote write of a managed deposit or submission claims its step at the
  store revision that the last validation read, so a change after that
  validation stops the write. A managed submission also checks that the current
  bundle is the bundle of its saved intent.

## Scientific delivery of a scoped study

`export` of a readiness, manuscript or round packet runs this delivery when the
study has a selected contract. The packet then also carries the artifacts that
its JSON artifacts name, and `inputs.json` is compact JSON.

- A contract can declare `scientific_delivery` projections for authored
  artifacts: `json_locators`, `text_spans`, `archive_members` and `npy_array`.
  Every required evidence locator keeps its exact value in a derivative. The
  bounds are 64 MiB for an artifact, 1,000 generic archive members, 20,000
  members of a declared NPY population, 8,000,000 numerical elements and
  512 MiB for the complete delivery. A bound that is exceeded stops the export
  before it creates the destination. Nothing is truncated.
- Delivered bytes must not contain the content of a private artifact: an
  authorization, acquisition evidence, preparer record or earlier review. The
  check reads raw bytes and the decoded string values and keys of JSON in every
  JSON encoding, down to depth 40. A nested acquisition role stays private when
  a public original is delivered. Bound round investigation captures keep
  their exact bytes.
- An exact public source that was first pending and later recaptured is
  delivered from its validated recapture, whatever the capture order.
- A declared JSON media type requires strict JSON. Undeclared bytes that begin
  with a bracket keep the depth bound. When a JSON decoder reads them, they
  must be strict JSON too; otherwise they are delivered as exact text, such as
  a plain-text transcript of an abstract page. Text that a decoder reads but
  that is not strict JSON, such as JSON with duplicate fields, is refused,
  because as text its escaped strings would not reach the privacy checks.
- A `json_locators` or `text_spans` derivative stores each selected value once,
  in `entries[i].value`. `entries[i].original_locator` and
  `locator_mapping[i].original_locator` carry the locator without its value
  and `locator_digest`, the SHA-256 of the complete declared locator in
  canonical JSON. The manifest carries each complete original locator. A
  manifest locator without a mapping item, including one whose value differs
  only in canonical form, is refused with `scientific_projection_incomplete`.
- The scientific target of a contract treats its claims, evidence and
  limitations as sets, so their order does not change the target digest.
- Pre-generated delivery bytes, such as the current claims of a marked scoped
  bundle, must sit at the content path of their bytes, else `artifact_corrupt`.
- One evaluation reuses its checked JSON parses, derivative locator mappings
  and parsed source readers, and a storage snapshot materializes each current
  record once. A new snapshot starts with new caches. This removes repeated
  work in large deliveries and can use more memory.

## Deposit disclosure

The disclosure at the end of a Zenodo description now states that the human
author "is responsible for its content". It no longer states that the author
reviewed the full content, which the command cannot verify.

## Upgrade notes

- Existing studies need no migration. Records written by 0.43.0 stay valid,
  and the new record kinds appear only when a study uses the new operations.
- The export of a study without a selected contract keeps its format. Its
  readiness packet adds the source-gap disclosure of the foundation, and its
  manuscript packet adds `source_gaps`; both are empty lists when the study
  deferred no source.
- A study that runs a local runtime of the v7 repairs can move to this
  release. Compared with the last local runtime (`b2724ae`), this release
  refuses undeclared text that a JSON decoder reads but that is not strict
  JSON, and it checks the path of pre-generated delivery bytes. The derivative
  files of a new export can differ from those of an export made by an earlier
  local runtime; the delivered packets stay as they were.
