# Exactory 0.69.11

This patch makes the manuscript citation check end URLs and DOIs by their context, accept URL schemes of any length,
and read unclosed bibliography entries in linear time.

## Why

The independent review of 0.69.10 found five defects.

- Every `](` ended a URL or a DOI as a Markdown link boundary. With an unrelated title,
  `doi={10.9999/x](arxiv:2609.14520)}` therefore cited `arxiv:2609.14520v1`.
- A URL scheme longer than 32 characters produced no URL, so the text of that URL could cite a work.
- URLs stopped at `<`. A `doi.org` URL for a legitimate SICI DOI, such as
  `10.1002/(sici)1097-0266(199708)18:7<509::aid-smj882>3.0.co;2-z`, therefore lost its DOI.
- A trailing `)`, `]` or `>` was always removed, so the DOI `10.1234/abc(def)` became `10.1234/abc(def`.
- Each unclosed entry scanned to the end of the file, so 10,000 lines of `@misc{k,` took about 100 seconds.

## What changed

- A URL scheme is the longest run of scheme characters directly before `://`, of any length.
- A URL or a DOI that starts right after `<` ends before the first `>`. One that starts right after `[` ends before the
  first `](`. Everywhere else `<`, `>`, `[`, `]` and `](` belong to the identifier.
- A trailing `.`, `,`, `;` or `:` is removed. A trailing `)`, `]` or `>` is removed only while the identifier holds more
  of it than of the matching opening bracket.
- An entry ends at its closing delimiter or at the next entry head, whichever comes first.
- The reading of URLs, DOIs, labels, `eprint` fields and old-style ids, the identifier sets, title matching and
  `find_citation_tokens` do not change.

These rules close the cases that the reviews of 0.69.5 to 0.69.10 reported.

## Upgrade

- A `doi.org` URL with `<` or `>` in its DOI, and a DOI with balanced parentheses, cite their work again.
- An identifier that holds `](` or `>` without an opening `[` or `<` before it now keeps that text, so the text no
  longer cites another work.
- When a Markdown link text holds a URL or a DOI after other words, the identifier runs into the link target, and the
  entry cites the work only by its title.
- The notes on BibTeX keys (since 0.69.8), on DOIs in publisher URLs (since 0.69.10) and on `already_cited` items
  (since 0.69.5) still apply. Publication bundles that are already recorded do not change.

## What stays the same

The problem citation projection, the reviewer prompts, the route-pinned transport and cost modules and
`review_state_evaluation.py` are unchanged.

## Checks

The new tests ran first against 0.69.10 and failed as expected (141 failed subtests; 10,000 unclosed entries took
about 100 seconds). After the change, with `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 PYTHONPATH=tests`, the modules
`test_research_citation_accounting`, `test_research_problem_citations`, `test_research_providers`,
`test_research_publication`, `test_research_publication_records`, `test_research_publication_files`,
`test_research_problem_delivery`, `test_math_problems_cli`, `test_codex`, `test_manifest` and `test_research_guidance`
(408 tests) passed on Python 3.12.8 and 3.9.6, and every performance case took less than one second.
`codex/generate.py --check` and `git diff --check` pass.

## Corrections in 0.69.12

The independent review of this release found that URLs and DOIs still ended at `{`, `}`, `"` and `\`, so text in a
braced group of a DOI value could cite a work, and that the statement "These rules close the cases that the reviews of
0.69.5 to 0.69.10 reported" did not cover that inherited case. The upgrade note on Markdown link texts is also too
strong: such an entry can lose its identifier evidence, but a URL whose query takes the tail still reads correctly.
Release 0.69.12 corrects the first point.
