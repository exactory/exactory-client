# Exactory 0.90.0

This release is the first part of Stage 2 of the capacity, continuity and openness designs. It follows 0.89.3. A
new problem openness chain uses the reading protocol v6, which has a finite projection and an answer that is a
finding. A refused output gets a replacement that carries its refusal, and every chain has a stored capacity
plan. Every code on which a series can end has a row in the registry of stop codes. The records, the designs, the
rulings and the reviews are in `misc/harness-improvement/20261113110022-every-open-record-and-review-capacity` of
the exactory repository.

## What changed

The reading protocol v6 and the openness finding (design D3, units UB1 to UB10):

- A new problem openness chain is a chain of `exactory-openness-reading-v6`. Its export writes the minimum of
  version 2 with `minimum_gaps` and `audit_forecast`. Chains of v5 keep their protocol.
- The first assignment of a v6 chain is admitted only when the projection of the chain fits its route. The
  projection is an upper bound of every request that the chain can still build. The allowance `record` is at
  least 300,000 serialized characters, and an identifier is charged at its reserved length of 120 characters. A
  chain whose projection does not fit stops before any call with the decision `review_chain_projection_over_limit`.
- The final answer of a v6 chain is a finding with its grounds, validated by `validate_finding`, with the UTC date
  of `searched_through`.
- The export binds the full reading of another record of the same work (a family reading, with `family_of`), the
  supporting readings that five fields of a bound search cite, and the accepted results that a disposition of a
  bound search cites. A work that is bound through a family reading is a decisive work.
- Both v6 prompts hold the guide to the fields of a search record.
- A v6 chain whose packet no longer matches the current records hands off to `export`. Its verified reading turns
  stay.
- `record-check` qualifies every stored openness finding under its own minimum version, and it does not rewrite
  the finding.

The capacity of a review (design D1):

- A new assignment of a planned chain stores `capacity_plan`: the room of the route divided among the fixed parts
  and the allowances. A request outside its plan is the harness defect `review_route_capacity_plan_violated`.
  `review-assignment --check` and the receipts report the plan, the forecast and the request measure.
- After a capacity event, `plan_capacity_recovery` builds the smaller task from the stored plan and its attempts,
  or returns a decision. One recovery table holds 38 pairs of kind and protocol.
- A single review above the start value of its kind is recorded as a task series from its first assignment.
- A packet that supplies a handle table lets the reviewer cite by handle. The harness expands each handle into the
  exact reference before every validator, and authentication repeats the expansion.
- A new bar, slate, result or session turn states the output limit and the answer bound of its turn in a
  versioned prompt paragraph.
- The inventory and the chain state of the reading protocol v7 are built. New chains stay on v6 until 0.91.0.

A refused output (design D2):

- The notice of a refused output is built in a whole form or in a bounded form (at most four entries and 45,000
  characters). A contract table names the 17 output checks and the fields that each one can show.
- A replacement in the same position, with the same reviewer, carries `output_refusal`: the diagnosis of every
  refused attempt of the position. `validate_replacement_origin` verifies it from the stored records. A third
  replacement of one position is refused.
- After a refused output, `review-series` plans the replacement along the measured size ladder. An exhausted
  position gets the repair handoff of its role.
- New assignments take the common prompt version 4 of the evidence grammar. A chain keeps its version.

The registry of stop codes and the ending of a series (design D2):

- `ci/series_code_reach.py` computes every code that `review-series` can emit. Every one of these codes has a
  registry row, and a test keeps the table of codes without a row empty.
- `review-series` returns only through its ending. Every ending with a code is retained in the store, or in
  `.exactory/series-endings/` when the store refuses the write. `record-check` reads both places.
- An assignment recorded on an unverified route is dead. `review-run` does not start it, and a dead head is
  replaced.

Searches, supplied texts and citations (design D3 and the triage of the open records):

- `search` takes `completes`: a search can complete an earlier one when the nine conditions of D3 hold.
- The new command `import-fulltext` keeps a PDF or a UTF-8 text that a person saved, with its SHA-256, its
  reported origin and a fixed note. Such a supplied text is admitted for a bundle, a complete original, a session
  delivery and an openness packet, and every delivery keeps its origin.
- A row disposition records the author's statement about one returned search row that cannot be imported. An
  arXiv search by `id_list` binds its query.
- A new problem or override export (schema `exactory-problem-export-v4`) takes the title and the author names of
  each citation from the registry assertion of its exact identifier.

The texts that a reviewer and an agent read in these units were written by the project's writer and reviewed at
effort max.

The first CI run of this release failed 44 tests. Three fixes are in the harness. `status` measures its elapsed
time from before the snapshot authentication. A stored assignment without `recorded_revision` is read as revision 0
when a later member of its position is looked up. A staged estimate counts the capacity notice of its frozen frame.
The other failures were test fixtures that lacked what the new contracts read, and expectations that the designs
changed on purpose. Incident ledger entries `release-0900-ci-failures-a` and `release-0900-ci-failures-b`.

## Compatibility

No file of a reviewer route's runtime identity changes, so no route becomes stale. Stored records, packets, prompts
and requests keep their meaning and their digests. A chain of v5 keeps its protocol. A recorded prompt keeps its
version. A stored openness chain that would be admitted under v6 only with a projection below its floor waits for
the reading protocol v7 of 0.91.0.
