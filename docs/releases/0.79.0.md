# Exactory 0.79.0

This release records the user's instruction that passes a gate, and lets the stage of a study move and a problem
post go on under it. It follows 0.78.0. It is phase B of the work recorded in
`misc/harness-improvement/20261106085323-override-of-harness-stops` of the exactory repository. GPT 6 Astra at
effort `xhigh` reviewed the design, and the owner authorized the implementation on 2026-11-06.

## Why

- Since 0.40.0, the user's instruction authorizes `exactory-draft deposit`, `exactory submit` and
  `exactory verify`: a store that refuses the receipt prints one `Managed record skipped` line, and the command
  runs. Nothing covered the steps between them. On 0.76.0, `exactory-lab state set` refused every forward stage
  after `literature` with `readiness_required` or `readiness_transition` and had no way on, `exactory-research
  gate` had none, and a problem whose result reviews had stopped could be posted only from files composed by hand.
- Two math-conjecturer records stopped in their result reviews on 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04, one with
  `review_input_too_large` after 22 turns and one on a 5.9 MB packet. The owner's written order and a
  hand-written composer got them through, with about 24 hours lost on the first. An external user has neither.
- The owner accepted this principle on 2026-11-06: with the user's instruction, any gate of a pipeline can be
  passed, and the fact is recorded. Without an instruction, nothing in this release passes a gate. The agent's own
  judgment is never the trigger, and a refusal that names the override command is not an instruction.

## What changed

- `exactory-research gate-override` records the user's instruction as `gate_override/<id>`. The payload names
  the gate, the path of a file under `context/` that holds the user's own words (a regular UTF-8 file, not a
  link, not empty, at most 64 KiB), a reason, and optionally the stop's code and one stage move of a study. The
  record keeps the instruction's path, text and stored artifact, the gate's unmet obligations at that revision
  as `{code, explanation}`, `authority: "user"` and `evidence: "reported_by_agent"`, because the harness checks
  the pinned bytes and not their authorship. With `gate` `post` it binds the current statement by the digest of
  its statement text and resolution criteria: a change to either needs a new override, and the digest does not
  cover the id, title, area, neighbours or formal statement. The command supports `--check`.
- `exactory-lab state set` permits a stage move when a recorded override names exactly that move, from the
  current stage to the same or a later stage. Closing a stage is its own move: `--status done` is the transition
  from the stage to itself, governed by the gate of that step, for `write` the `manuscript` gate. The store does
  not expire a recorded transition: after a return to an earlier stage, the same move passes again. That fact
  gives no permission. Whether the user's words cover the second pass is decided by those words, not by the
  store; an instruction that answered one refusal is not standing permission, and when the words do not cover
  it, the agent reports the stop. One instruction file can back several overrides. The decision log is a step,
  not a stop: a decision recorded with `exactory-lab decide` satisfies no gate, and closing a stage still needs
  one.
- No gate reads an override. The fourteen `gate` actions report the same before and after one, and
  `manuscript`, `problem-posted` and the managed deposit refuse as before. An override creates no review,
  score, acceptance or decision record. Work done after one keeps its exploratory label.
- `exactory-research export --kind problem-override` composes the post files of a problem from the current
  statement and the value record when a `post` override for that statement text and resolution criteria
  exists (`gate_override_required` otherwise; `problem_value_missing` without the value record). It reads no
  review and no decision; its manifest has `decision_id` and `problem_evidence_digest` null, the `override_id`
  and `gate: "bypassed"`. The post then goes through `exactory post-problem`; the agent reads the posted problem
  back, stores that response with `artifact`, and records the post with `problem-post-external`, with that
  artifact as `evidence` and the server's `createdAt` as `posted_at`. `problem-posted` refuses the export.
- `status` and `next` list the recorded overrides under `overrides`, with the quoted instruction and the
  obligation codes, and the local entries under `unrecorded_overrides`. The `--summary` views show at most
  three of each, the text cut at 120 characters and at most six codes, within their existing byte bounds. The
  output views write each record to `final/overrides/<id>.json`. The `Managed record skipped` line of
  `exactory-draft deposit`, `exactory submit` and `exactory verify` ends with `Overrides: <ids>.`
- The deposit package exports the kind: an archive that `exactory-draft package` builds from the pinned
  manuscript bundle after the override holds the override records and their instruction artifacts, and not the
  local log. Those bytes become public when the archive is attached with `--sources` and the deposit is
  published; under those conditions the user's instruction text is in the public deposit archive.
- A deposit made while the store or the local log holds an override adds one paragraph to the record's
  description on Zenodo, after the authorship disclosure. It says what the record shows and what it does not:
  "This paper was deposited from a workspace of a research harness that checks each step of the work before
  the next one. Some of these checks require independent reviews. The record of that workspace holds entries
  for these checks: readiness, publication. A readable entry says that the user of the session instructed the
  work to go on without waiting for that check. The agent that ran the work recorded the entry. An entry named
  unknown could not be read. The record does not show who that user was, whether the entry was used, or
  whether the check was completed later." A local entry that could not be read is named `unknown` among the
  checks. The description without an override is byte for byte what 0.78.0 sent.
- When the store refuses the record with `corrupt_state` or `storage_io`, the command without `--check`
  appends the override to `.exactory/unrecorded-overrides.jsonl` and returns `{recorded: false, path}`. The
  entry holds the instruction text and its SHA-256, and no obligations. It permits no move, and it is not
  imported into the store later; when the store opens again, the agent records the override in the store. The
  log is published read-only and its lock file stays private. An entry that cannot be read is shown as
  `unreadable-line-<n>` with gate `unknown`.
- `docs/research-workflow.md` has a shared section, "Stops and overrides": how to report a stop in one report,
  what counts as an instruction, the command sequence, the direct paths, what stays outside an override, and
  what the user's words become. Eleven skills link to it, each with one line placed where a gate refuses:
  ai-science, math-conjecturer, verify, write, deposit, submit, experiment, ideate, literature-review,
  evaluate and cohort. The verify skill keeps its rule against bypassing on the agent's own judgment and
  names the recorded way on the user's instruction. The CLI reference describes the command and the export,
  and the example catalog has `gate-override`.
- The regression ledger has two new `real_shape` entries, `override-stage-transition` with eight tests and
  `override-problem-composer` with four. `tests/test_gate_override.py` has 30 tests.
- Not in this release: the classes of stops and the automatic pass of a stop that the harness classifies as
  its own defect (phase C), and the math-solver controller (phase D). Until then, every stop is reported, and
  only the user's instruction passes it.

Records written by earlier releases do not change; the kind `gate_override` is added only when the command
records it. No migration is necessary.

## Checks

- The 20 test modules that touch the last fixes and the texts pass on Python 3.12.8 and 3.9.6 with 452 tests each, on
  the final text.
- A wider run of 1,674 tests per version, before the last four fixes, left no unresolved failure. Twenty of those tests
  could not open a socket in the sandbox of that run. Their module, `tests.test_draft_problem_attachment`, passes with
  150 tests on both versions on this machine.
- `codex/generate.py --check` and `git diff --check` pass. No protected module changes.
- A study ran through the real commands from `experiment` to `complete` on one instruction, with nine overrides. Without
  an override, `state set` refuses with `readiness_required`. At `complete`, the five gates that were checked still
  refuse, `status` lists the unmet obligations, and no review, score or acceptance record exists. The only decision
  records are those of `exactory-lab decide`.
- While that review was open, the same run showed that a damaged line in the local log stopped `status`, `next` and a
  deposit. This is fixed, with tests.
- `record-check` and the record fingerprint against 0.78.0 ran on 4 of the 21 live records before the pull request.
  All 4 pass, and their fingerprints are the same. The other 17 run in the background.
- The texts were written by Fable 5.1 and reviewed once by Astra 6 at `max`. Its findings 1 to 11 are applied, as is
  its twelfth, except for the removal of the opening history of this note, which is the form of the earlier notes.
