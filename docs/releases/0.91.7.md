# Exactory 0.91.7

With this release, a refusal of the check `evidence.shape` names the refused entries of its array and states the
accepted forms of an entry. A commit of another command during the capture of a probe does not change the recorded
outcome of that probe. The ending of `review-series` shows each answer with the steps that its plan built. Two
skills state the order of the steps of a stale-route handoff, and the general tests of the CI run in eight shards.
The release follows 0.91.6. Its records are `20261113110022-every-open-record-and-review-capacity`,
`20261115234509-bar-slot-ends-on-evidence-form-and-route-input-limit` and
`20261116013236-probe-capture-fails-when-another-writer-commits`, under `misc/harness-improvement/` of the exactory
repository.

## What changed

The refusal of an evidence entry:

- The check `evidence.shape` examines the form of each entry of an evidence array in the answer of a reviewer. An
  entry has one of three forms: an artifact descriptor, a source link or a record.
- Before this release, the check stopped at the first refused entry. Its refusal gave the path of the array and
  one message, and the notice of a replacement had empty details.
- In one study, a bar reviewer wrote two refused forms. The first was a descriptor under the member name
  `artifact`. The second had the members of a source link as the outermost members of the entry. In three answers
  of one turn position, every entry of every evidence array had a refused form (45 of 45, 45 of 45 and 15 of 15).
  The position ended without a bar.
- Now the check examines every entry. The code and the message are those of the first refused entry, as before.
  The details add `refused_indexes`, `refused_count` and `notice`.
- `refused_indexes` gives the positions of the refused entries in the array at `path`, counted from 0. It holds at
  most 100 positions. `refused_count` gives the number of all refused entries.
- `notice` is a fixed text of 1,850 characters. It states the three forms with one example object for each. It
  says how to write the two forms of that study, and it says which source links are accepted.
- For a review of a problem record, the text has 2,072 characters. A record reference of such a review can omit
  its digest, and the text says so.
- The check also refuses the array itself in two cases: the value is no array, or the array is empty. The details
  of that refusal hold `notice` alone.
- Two arrays have another grammar: the evidence of a readiness check in the `support` member of a study result
  review, and the evidence of a verification. When the check refuses entries of such an array, it gives the two
  numbers. It never gives `notice` for such an array.
- Some refusals of the check did not name their array. Now `path` is `/reassessment/findings/<i>/evidence` for a
  reassessment finding, `/evidence` for a problem adjudication or a staged adjudicator, and
  `/checks/<i>/evidence` for a verification check. Before this release, these refusals had the path
  `/reassessment` or no path.
- The notice of a replacement carries the three details. As a result, the reviewer gets the positions and the
  forms with its next request. This also applies to a refusal that an earlier release recorded.
- The CLI reference describes the three details with the member `output_refusal` and with the refusal records.

Probes and other writers:

- `exactory-lab probe` runs a program as a probe. After the program ends, the worker of the probe does the
  capture. The capture writes one record for each stream and for each new or changed output file. The launcher
  then records the outcome.
- Before this release, each of these writes was a compare-and-swap on the revision of the whole store. A write
  had at most 8 attempts within 8 seconds, and a commit of another command during an attempt refused that attempt.
- When the write for an output file stayed refused, the capture recorded the refusal as the outcome of the probe.
  That outcome was `failed`, with the observation "Probe output capture failed: stale_revision" or
  "Probe output capture failed: store_busy". The record of a probe is immutable, so the store lost the exit
  status of the program.
- In one study, a program exited with status 0 and got such a record, without outputs. At that time, the capture
  of a second probe committed its records.
- When the write of the intent or of the launch stayed refused, `exactory-lab probe` was refused with
  `stale_revision` or `store_busy` before the program ran. This occurred for probes that started together.
- Now each record that the launcher and the worker write for a probe commits against the records that its write
  reads. It does not commit against the revision of the store. A commit that changes none of those records does
  not refuse the write. When a commit changes one of them, the write is prepared again.
- Each write waits for a busy store, or for a changed record, for at most 600 seconds. The finish of a reviewer
  call has the same wait, and the two read one definition.
- A capture does not record a refusal of the store or of the artifact store as an outcome. Such a capture ends
  without an outcome. The files of the run stay, and the probe keeps its reservation.
- An operating-system error of the artifact store during a capture, for example a full disk, is such a refusal.
  `exactory-lab probe` then ends with the error `probe_io`. When the cause is gone, the same command with the same
  `--id` records the outcome.
- When the worker cannot write a record after the wait, it ends. The launcher then does the capture itself, with
  the same wait. When the launcher cannot write a record after the wait, `exactory-lab probe` is refused with
  `store_busy`, and `details.cause` holds the last refusal.
- The message of that refusal says what was recorded and what the same command with the same `--id` does next.
  It has three forms:
  - The intent was not recorded. No record of the probe exists, and the program did not run. The same command
    records the intent and runs the program.
  - The launch was not recorded. The intent is recorded and keeps its reservation, and the program did not run.
    The same command records the probe as `interrupted`, without outputs, and ends the reservation. To run the
    program after that, use a new `--id`.
  - A later record was not written. The probe has no outcome and keeps its reservation. The same command records
    the outcome from the kept files and does not run the program again.
- The worker cannot read some outputs safely: a link, a path that leaves the output directory, or a file that it
  cannot read. Such an output gives a failed capture, as before. The outcome now also holds
  `capture_failure: {code, program_exit_status}`, which gives the cause and the exit status of the program.
- An earlier release can leave an outcome file with one of the two observations above and no record of that
  outcome. The launcher does not accept such a file as an outcome. It does the capture again from the kept files.
- The commands `exactory-research probe-intent` and `exactory-research probe` keep their compare-and-swap on the
  revision that the caller gives.
- The CLI reference describes the wait, the refusal `store_busy` and its three messages. The `experiment` skill
  says what to do after that refusal.

The steps of an ending:

- When `review-series` ends on a stop, its last line holds a decision. Each answer of the decision lists its
  steps. The ending completes one of these steps, the step of the ending (`details.step`), with the arguments of
  its command.
- From 0.90.0 to 0.91.6, the ending then copied this completed step over every step of every answer that had the
  same command.
- For the stop `review_turn_failures_exhausted` of a bar, a slate or a result review, the answer
  "Keep this route" then began with a `review-context` event of kind `route_retired`. That step is the first
  step of the answer "Move to another route". The plan gives "Keep this route" a `review-context` event of kind
  `observation` as its first step.
- For the stop `review_pending_delivery_over_plan`, each `select-main-capture` step named the first pending
  source. In one study, the seven capture steps of an ending named one source.
- Now only the step of the ending gets its completed form. Each other step keeps its own id, payload, `chooses`
  and prerequisites, and it still gets the arguments of its command.

The order of the steps of a stale-route handoff:

- The handoff `review_route_runtime_stale` of a head with a refused answer can hold `details.renewal` with
  `admitted: false`. `details.steps` then lists the steps of the stale-route procedure for the role of the head.
- The `ai-science` skill and the `math-conjecturer` skill now say that `details.steps` lists these steps in the
  order to take them.
- For a slot with history, the sentences of both skills name the new route record and its probe before the
  retirement of the stale route. `details.steps` gives the retirement first. In the order of the sentences, the
  context repair is refused with `review_context_repair_unverified`, and it then needs one more probe.

The CI:

- The general tests ran in four shards for each Python version, and each shard has a limit of 180 minutes. In the
  run of 0.91.4 on `main`, the CI cancelled shards 0 and 2 on Python 3.9 at that limit. No test of these two
  shards failed before the limit.
- The general tests now run in eight shards for each Python version. The job that examines the results of the
  shards expects eight of them.

Incident ledger: 3 entries set to 0.91.7.

## Effects and limits

The refusal notice:

- A notice entry of `evidence.shape` is larger than before by the text of `notice` and by the positions. With 100
  positions of seven digits, its details have approximately 2,900 characters, or 3,100 for a review of a problem
  record. The limit of the details is 4,000 characters, so the notice keeps them in full. A notice holds at most
  four entries.
- `review-assignment` measures the request of a replacement with its notice, as before. It tries the form
  `whole`, then the form `bounded`, then the smaller task. As a result, a replacement near the limit of its task
  or of its route can go from `whole` to `bounded`. When no form fits, the refusal is `review_input_too_large`,
  and the turn position is exhausted.
- A test fixture shows this effect for a study bar at the smallest task level, 15,000 characters. On 0.91.6 the
  replacement with the bounded notice has 14,890 characters and fits. With this release the same replacement has
  17,053 characters. The plan of the position is then the handoff `review_slot_exhausted`. Its details name
  `review_input_too_large`, and its step is `review-assignment` for a new reviewer.
- A new refusal record with `notice` holds that text in full. The receipt of `review-run` and the series view
  show the text.
- This release changes no prompt. The prompt of a study review states no form of an evidence entry. As a result,
  a study reviewer gets the forms with the notice, after a refused answer.
- One answer can hold arrays with two record forms. This occurs in the answer of a problem adjudicator under a
  context repair. A reassessment finding accepts a record without its digest, and its notice says that the digest
  is optional. The array `/evidence` of the same answer requires the digest. An adjudicator that omits the digest
  there is refused again. The prompts of a problem adjudicator ask for the digest.

Probes:

- The wait of 600 seconds applies to each record, and not to the command. A capture of n new or changed output
  files writes n + 2 records, and the outcome is one more record. When the worker ends after its wait, the
  launcher waits again for each record. As a result, one command can wait for longer than 600 seconds.
- While a write waits, the probe has no outcome and keeps its reservation. A second `exactory-lab probe` with the
  same `--id` answers `pending`. A plan that must account for the run of the probe is refused with
  `preplan_probe_pending`. The intent of another probe can be refused with `resource_budget_exhausted`.
- After a refused launch, the same command records the probe as `interrupted`. That record counts one unknown
  usage of wall seconds in the experiment account, although the program did not run.
- A probe that an earlier release recorded as `failed` with one of the two observations keeps that record. This
  release adds no command that replaces the record of a probe.

Endings:

- A stop that 0.90.0 to 0.91.6 recorded keeps its content, with the copied steps. Run `review-series` on the same
  head again. Its last line then shows the steps that the plan built, with the same stop id.
- When an agent took the first step of "Keep this route" from such an ending, that step recorded the retirement
  of the route. This release does not change that record.

## Compatibility

No file of the runtime identity of a reviewer route changes, and no reviewer prompt changes. As a result, no route
becomes stale. Stored records, packets, prompts and requests keep their bytes and their digests.

Refusals of `evidence.shape`:

- The check gives each stored output the result that it gave before: the same code and message, or no refusal.
  The record fingerprint holds refusal codes and no details of a refusal, so this change does not move it.
- An entry after a refused entry can make the check raise a Python error. The check counts such an entry as one
  more refused entry, so the refusal of the first refused entry stays.
- A stored refusal record keeps its details. A stored replacement whose notice entry has empty details stays
  verified.
- A new diagnosis of a stored output differs in its details. They hold the three new members, and `path` names
  the array of a reassessment finding, of an adjudication or of a verification check.
- The packet of a new replacement differs: its notice entry holds the three details.

Probes:

- The records of a probe keep their members, their request ids and their operation names. The record of a
  complete capture has the members that 0.91.6 gives it.
- `capture_failure` is a new optional member of an outcome. No outcome of an earlier release holds it. A new
  result packet delivers the member in the row of a disclosed run under `program_probes`. A new problem packet
  delivers it in a probe record.
- A probe that an earlier release left without an outcome gets its outcome from the same command with its `--id`.
- One refusal has a new form. Before this release, `exactory-lab probe` was refused with `stale_revision` or
  `store_busy` after 8 seconds, with the message of the store. Now the refusal is `store_busy` after the wait,
  with one of the three messages and `details.cause`.

Endings:

- The identity of an ending does not change. A stop that an earlier release recorded is the same ending after
  this release.
- The last line of `review-series` differs for the two stops above: each answer holds the steps of the plan.
