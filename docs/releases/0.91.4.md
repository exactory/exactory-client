# Exactory 0.91.4

This release corrects what a new v7 openness chain carries from earlier chains, and the handoff of a stopped chain
to its successor. No command records an assignment on a stale route record (a route record that pins another
reviewer runtime than the installed one) any more. A refused session turn on such a record gets its replacement on
another route record of the same identity. An accepted output correction stays selected when the reviewer runtime
changes or a later record arrives. The release follows 0.91.3. Its records are
`misc/harness-improvement/20261113110022-every-open-record-and-review-capacity` and
`misc/harness-improvement/20261112072628-bar-final-refused-for-part-citation-with-whole-text-offsets` of the
exactory repository.

## What changed

What a new v7 chain carries:

- A new v7 openness chain takes over reading turns of earlier chains under v5, v6 or v7. These chains have the
  same reviewer, author, statement, formulations and route identity. An entry is a verified excerpt or a delivered
  passage answer of such a turn.
- Before this release, the selection carried a turn only when it took a range or a completed search from that
  turn. A turn that delivered shared search fields or an earlier part of a search record was left out, with its
  notes and its entries. On a copy of one live store, 12 of the 57 turns of a stopped chain were left out, with
  164 entries. No report named them. These numbers come from 0.91.1, and 0.91.2 and 0.91.3 make the same
  selection.
- The selection of a new chain is now of version 2, `exactory-openness-carried-selection-v2`. When the selection
  takes anything from a chain, it carries every ready reading turn of that chain with its notes. A ready reading
  turn has an observed, authenticated output in a verified context. An entry is carried when its own source is an
  unchanged unit of the new packet. If a later chain read the characters of an entry again, the entry is
  superseded and stays with its own chain.
- On the same copy, a prototype of this rule carried all 57 turns of the stopped chain and all 1,088 of its
  entries. It took the same 63 ranges and 20 searches as 0.91.1. A test on a fixture of the same shape checks that
  all 57 turns and every entry are carried.
- The receipt of the first assignment, also with `--check`, holds `carried_readings.outputs`. For each earlier
  chain that qualifies, it gives the ready reading turns, the carried turns, and the entries under `carried`,
  `superseded` and `source_changed`.
- The verification of a stored selection of version 2 has two parts. In the first part, the harness reads the
  store as it stood before the first assignment, from the history of the store. It makes the selection again on
  that state, and the stored turns must equal the result. In the second part, every earlier chain that gives a
  carried turn must authenticate under the records as they stand now. So a selection that lacks a turn, the end
  of a chain, a whole chain or an entry does not verify.
- A v7 chain that 0.91.0 to 0.91.3 started keeps its selection of version 1, with its bytes and its verification.
- The forecast of a new chain can be higher than before. The new chain carries more entries, and it reads the
  carried entries that do not fit the pool of its final. On the copy, `review-assignment --check` forecasts 13
  calls, where 0.91.1 forecast 12.
- When a review assignment of a store outside managed research holds a selection of version 2, the store keeps the
  index of its record versions. A managed store keeps this index already. Every read of such a store builds the
  index. On the copy, a read took 3.2 seconds with the index and 2.2 to 2.3 seconds without it. The index held
  50,206 record versions in 32 MB. On the test fixture of the same shape, a read took 0.85 seconds with the index
  and 0.72 seconds without it. There the index kept about 8 MB more in memory. Every other store is read as before.

The handoff of a stopped chain:

- When the next request of a v5 or v6 chain cannot fit, the chain stops. `review-series` then gives the handoff
  `export` when a protocol admits the projected packet of the next export.
- Before this release, the projection upgraded the minimum of a v5 packet to version 2 and kept the evidence of the
  old packet. When a bound search names a reading, a minimum of version 2 binds that reading, and the projected
  packet did not carry it. The same held for a family reading that the minimum adds to the decisive readings. The
  stopped chain then ended with `openness_reading_incomplete` in place of the handoff `export`. On the same copy of
  one live store, the minimum bound two readings that searches name. The stopped chain of 57 turns ended in this
  way.
- The projected packet now carries every record that its minimum binds. `export` builds this evidence with the
  same function. On the copy, a prototype of the fix gave the handoff `export` with a v7 successor that carries
  both earlier chains. A test on a fixture of the same shape checks this handoff.

What the selection reads:

- The selection now compares what the records hold before it reads an artifact of an earlier assignment. A
  candidate is the first assignment of an earlier reading chain. It has the reviewer, the author and the route
  identity of the new chain. Of a candidate, the selection reads the native packet. When the statement and the
  formulations are those of the new chain, it also reads the packet of the assignment. For a chain under v5, v6 or
  v7, it then reads the evidence of the native packet and the turns of the chain.
- The selection reads no artifact of any other assignment. Examples are a single-turn review, a replacement of a
  first turn and a chain on another route identity. For a chain under another reviewer, the report of the carried
  turns reads its artifacts for its own rows (below).
- The first assignment of a v7 chain is refused while the artifact store cannot give one of these artifacts:
  1. The native packet of an earlier turn of a reading chain on the same route identity, whatever its reviewer
     and its statement.
  2. The stored capacity plan of such a turn over the same statement.
  3. The packet of the first turn of an earlier reading chain with the same reviewer, author, route identity,
     statement and formulations, under any reading protocol.
  4. For such a chain under v5, v6 or v7, the evidence of its native packet and every artifact that the
     observation of its turns reads.
- The admission of v7 reads the first two since 0.91.0. Under 0.91.0 to 0.91.3, the selection passed over a chain
  whose packet or evidence the store did not give. Now `review-assignment` and its check end with the refusal of
  the store before any record. When v6 starts the chain, a missing artifact of the last two does not stop the
  assignment. Its receipt then holds the refusal of the store under `carried_readings.refusal`.
- A finding of a v7 chain that 0.91.4 or later started is stale while the store cannot give one of the last two
  artifacts. The same holds for the native packet of the first turn of an earlier reading chain with the same
  reviewer, author and route identity. The finding is current again when the exact bytes are back.
- The report of the carried turns has a new member, `unavailable_chains`. A row names an earlier reading chain with
  the author and the route identity of the new chain, and another reviewer. The store did not give the packet of
  that chain, or evidence of its native packet that the new packet does not hold. The first assignment goes on,
  and the new chain carries none of the turns of that chain. The member is present only when it holds a row.
- A native packet whose `openness_minimum` is no object, or lacks its statement or its neighbours, makes the
  earlier assignment no candidate. Before this release, the selection then ended with a `TypeError` or a
  `KeyError`. The commands do not record such a packet.

Stale route records and the replacement of a refused turn:

- A route record is stale when the runtime that it pins is not equal to the installed reviewer runtime, as
  `review-run` compares them.
- Before this release, a refused turn whose route record was stale got its replacement on that record. The call then
  ended with `review_route_runtime_stale`, and the assignment stayed as the latest assignment of its turn position.
  `review-assignment` refused the same replacement on a new route record with the same adapter, model, endpoint and
  configuration. On a copy of another live store, the series of a bar review ended with the handoff `review-route`
  and made no reviewer call.
- Now no command records an assignment on a stale route record. `review-assignment` refuses it with
  `review_route_runtime_stale` before any record, also when `review-series` runs the command. This holds for a first
  turn, a next turn, a replacement, a context repair and the child of a staged review. The details name the route
  record in `route_id`. A retired route record keeps the refusal of its retirement, `review_route_retired`.
- For a route whose installed runtime cannot be read (for example, while the Codex CLI is missing), a new
  assignment is recorded as before. `review-run` then refuses it with `review_runtime_missing`.
- Under `record-check`, the reading of an HTTPS route holds only its adapter and the hash of its transport file.
  These two members are compared with the same two members of the pinned runtime. So a change of the host, the
  platform or the Python version of an HTTPS route shows at the command and not in the check.
- A stale route record that is also below the capacity floor now gives `review_route_runtime_stale` in place of
  `review_route_capacity_below_floor`. The capacity probe that this stop names cannot run on a stale record.
- A session turn is a turn of a `bar`, `slate`, `result` or `adjudicator` review. It is not staged, not delivered in
  parts, and not the first turn of a context repair. When the route record of a refused session turn is stale, its
  replacement now runs on another route record. That record has the same adapter, model, endpoint and
  configuration, pins the installed runtime, is not retired and is verified by a probe. The earlier turn of the
  session, when it has one, must be verified as `review-run` requires it.
- `review-series` plans this replacement on the latest verified route record of that kind. When the store lacks such
  a record, its probe or its capacity evidence, the plan is the handoff `review_route_runtime_stale`. Its
  `details.renewal.lacks` is `record`, `probe` or `capacity`, and `details.step` holds the command. A capacity probe
  is a paid call, and the owner authorizes it first. When no such record can carry the replacement, the plan is the
  step of the role under `review_slot_exhausted`. `details.renewal.unserved.reason` then says `limit`,
  `probes_used` or `probe_unavailable`.
- The replacement keeps the reviewer, the slot and the turn position of the refused turn. The position, its two
  replacements and the list of refusals in the notice are counted across both route records. A later turn takes the
  route record of the replacement.
- Every other refused turn on a stale route record keeps the stale-route procedure. This applies to the first turn
  of a context repair, a turn of a series in parts and a turn of a reading chain. It also applies to a session turn
  whose earlier turn is no longer verified. Their handoff has `details.renewal.admitted` false and lists the steps
  of the procedure under `details.steps`, before any record. Before this release, the series recorded the
  replacement first and then ended with one step of the procedure.
- The messages of `review_route_runtime_stale`, of this handoff and of `review_refusal_replacement_mismatch` say
  what to do in each case.

Output corrections and the reviewer runtime:

- An accepted output correction now verifies against the runtime that its route record pins. Before this release,
  it verified against the installed runtime. As a result, the correction lost its selection after an update of
  the reviewer CLI or of a transport module. A change of the program path and the removal of the program had the
  same effect. For an HTTPS route, a change of the host name, of the platform or of the Python version had the
  same effect. The assignment then showed `review_output_refused`.
- `review-output-correction` now compares the installed runtime with the pin of the route before it records the
  invocation. It also reads the reviewer CLI of a Codex route or a Claude route. On a stale route record, the
  command is refused with `review_route_runtime_stale`. Without the reviewer CLI, the command is refused with
  `review_runtime_missing`. Nothing is recorded, and the correction stays available. Before this release, such a
  command recorded a failed correction and used the one correction of the assignment.
- A correction answer from a call on the pinned runtime is accepted when the installed runtime changes before the
  answer is recorded. Before this release, that answer was recorded as `invalid`.
- A new test lists every function that reads the installed reviewer runtime, with its purpose. A function that
  reads the runtime without a declared purpose fails the test.

Output corrections and later records:

- An accepted output correction now verifies against the review records as they were before its call started.
  Before this release, each read admitted the correction again on the records of that time. As a result, a later
  record removed the correction. Examples are a route retirement, a probe that did not verify, a contamination
  event and a later assignment in the slot. The assignment then showed `review_output_refused` and no output.
- Such a later record now shows in the state of the assignment, as it shows for an answer that needed no
  correction. If a route retirement names an assignment in the lineage of a context repair, the state shows
  `review_context_repair_stale`. Any other route retirement adds no obligation. A probe that does not verify adds
  `review_context_unverified`. A contamination event adds `review_context_contaminated`. A successor in the slot
  adds `review_assignment_replaced`, and the successor receives the corrected output as its history.
- Before this release, such a later record also made the refusal planner plan a refusal replacement of the
  corrected assignment. The harness admitted that replacement, and the replacement discarded the accepted
  correction. The planner now stops, and the harness refuses the replacement.
- If another session writes a record during a correction call, the harness still accepts the answer. Before this
  release, such a record made the answer `invalid`, and the assignment lost its one correction.
- If an output correction ran on another route record than its assignment, the state of the assignment also reads
  the route state of that record.
- The stop `review_composition_pending` of a staged review now reads the stored correction records of a task. It
  applies while a correction call is pending and after a correction is accepted. For a pending call, its message
  says to reconcile the call before the composition is read. After an accepted correction, it says to authorize a
  new root, as before. Before this release, the stop read the selected correction. So it did not apply while a call
  was pending, while the installed runtime differed from the recorded one, or after a later record. No command
  records a correction on a staged task, so no stored review changes.
- The check of a recorded visual route transition now reads the earlier review records through the same view. The
  view does not copy the records. As a result, a store with more than one transition renders its PDF pages less
  often.

Tests:

- Six tests in `tests/test_visual_assignment_integration.py` and
  `tests/test_review_staged_integration_partitions.py` met the new admission check. Their route records pinned a
  runtime that the tests did not install, or no runtime. Five of them failed only on a machine with the Codex CLI,
  and one failed on every machine. The fixtures now install the runtime that they pin, and every assertion is kept.
  Both modules pass with and without the Codex CLI.
- The tests of archived stores whose route record pins an obsolete runtime now expect `review_route_runtime_stale`
  for a new assignment, before the capacity stop.

of output corrections, the second writer's pass and the merge of 0.91.3. It ran 9,305 tests with six failures. All

request runs the full suites on Python 3.9, 3.12 and 3.14.

## Compatibility

No file of a reviewer route's runtime identity changes, and no reviewer prompt changes, so no route becomes stale.
Stored records, packets, prompts and requests keep their meaning and their digests. A stored selection of version 1
keeps its bytes and its verification. An assignment that an earlier release recorded on a stale route record keeps
its record. A replacement that 0.90.0 to 0.91.3 recorded keeps its record and its verification.

In one live store, an accepted output correction stands on a route record that pins another runtime than the
installed one. With this release, the assignment of that correction is ready again with its corrected output. On a
copy of that store, the record fingerprint differs in the `assignment_state` of that one assignment and in no other
entry. The store holds no later record of the kinds above, so the rule of later records changes nothing more there.

These refusals are new:

- `review-assignment` refuses an assignment on a stale route record. Record and probe a route record with the same
  adapter, model, endpoint and configuration, and assign on it. A refused first assignment takes the same id there.
  For a refused turn, run `review-series` and take the step that it plans.
- `review-output-correction` refuses a stale route record. Record and probe a route record of the same identity,
  and run the command again with its id as `route_id`.
- `review-output-correction` refuses a route whose reviewer CLI is missing. Install the reviewer CLI, and run the
  command again.
- The first assignment of a v7 chain is refused while the store cannot give an artifact that the selection reads.
  Restore the exact bytes of that artifact, and assign again.
- A staged composition stops with `review_composition_pending` while a correction call of its task is pending.
  Reconcile the call first.
