# Exactory 0.75.0

This release delivers the three items that 0.74.0 left for it: the cost of `status` on review-heavy records, the
closure of review work per posted statement, and Codex hooks that survive a plugin update. It also closes the other
open items of the 0.73 effort. The release follows 0.74.1. The work is recorded in
`misc/harness-improvement/20261105155433-open-items-after-0-73-6` of the exactory repository. Each decision was made
in consultation with GPT 6 Astra at effort `ultra`.

## Why

- `status` took minutes on review-heavy records. To authenticate a recorded request, it built the request again
  after every reduction step and every source-capacity trial. Snapshot replay also encoded each stored event again
  after it had validated the parts of the event.
- Since 0.73.6 a post ended all review work of its record, also for statements that the post did not cover.
- Codex skips a changed plugin hook until the user trusts it again, so the exactory hooks stopped working after each
  plugin update. A running session also lost its code when an update removed the old plugin cache.
- A reviewer could not cite the evidence of a scoped round packet as it was delivered. A late HTTPS location of a
  read PDF needed a new reading. Pinned files that no record referenced did not reach a native review packet.
  `make-the-proof-constructive` became ineligible after its own first move.
- A refresh withheld more evidence bodies than the route limit required. A nested visual repair could not fit its
  history. The Codex adapters reported the requested model and effort without checking the actual turn.
- DOI-only abstract requests did not find related captured abstracts, and a generic publisher excerpt could not
  prove a complete abstract. A qualification with binary evidence settled a request on a route that cannot deliver
  that evidence.
- Smaller defects: a user-authorized deposit did not report its state apart from the managed stage; a round
  accepted a `revised` marker that did not bind the opening claim; a BOM-free UTF-16 or UTF-32 body escaped the
  privacy check; a valid artifact digest at a manifest boundary was reported as a corrupt manifest; the base
  verification prompt did not state its stance, band and sampling rules.

## What changed

- For ordinary requests on `codex_cli_v1` and `claude_cli_v1`, the authentication of a recorded request tracks the
  exact encoded lengths while it fits the request and builds the request once per fit order. Other
  representations use the pinned builder. All five fit orders, request bytes, refusal order and reports stay the
  same. Snapshot replay hashes each event from its already validated canonical segments: on a copy of a study with
  1,144 revisions, the replay takes 52 s of CPU time instead of 78 to 80 s. On a copy of the bunkbed record,
  `status` takes 80 s of CPU time instead of 240 s, with the same report.
- After a post, review work is closed per statement. A chain bound to the posted statement, by its
  `statement_id` or by the claim of its native openness packet, is `done`, and new work on it is refused with
  `problem_already_posted`. A chain of another statement continues. A chain bound to no statement is done when the
  post was recorded after the chain's first assignment; a post records its local creation revision for this. A
  record without a post plans as in 0.74.0.
- Managed Codex hooks: an administrator installs a stable dispatcher with `bin/exactory-runtime install` and
  publishes each reviewed release with `publish`. Codex trusts managed hooks by policy, so a plugin update needs no
  new review in `/hooks`, and a session keeps the release it started with until it ends. The procedure and its
  checks are in `docs/testing/codex-managed-hooks.md`. It needs administrator rights, and the acceptance test on
  the host is still open.
- `round-review` accepts a projected evidence reference copied unchanged from the delivered round packet of the
  decision under review. It maps the reference to the native evidence and keeps both in the record.
- `problem-citation-locator` (new command) records the HTTPS location of the retained original of an existing
  reading, without a new reading.
- Every pinned input of `search review-packet` reaches the native packet by its exact digest, without proof credit.
- `make-the-proof-constructive` continues after its own mode move on the same claim while its other required
  conditions hold, until the strategy ends with `fail`. A new test pins the strategies whose required entry
  questions read the component that they move, and the question numbers and markers of this strategy.
- A new ordinary review assignment on a fitted text route, other than a complete-source or staged one, records the
  restoration policy
  `exactory-review-request-restoration-v1`. After the reductions, it gives back complete current bodies in packet
  order while the encoded request fits. `record-check` and `ci/record_fingerprint.py` rebuild such requests under
  the recorded policy. Earlier assignments rebuild their requests unchanged.
- A context repair of a visual review records its authenticated ancestry and delivers the scientific history once.
- New plain and framed Codex reviews retain the native rollout. A probe, attempt or staged observation that carries
  the marker `codex_invocation` authenticates only with one `turn_context` that shows the route model and the
  effort `high`. Records without the marker keep their earlier authentication.
- DOI abstract requests report related captured abstracts as alternatives, with their provenance. A family alias
  never stands for the exact version. `import-response` takes `publisher_capture` with the format
  `springer-meta-json-v1` for a complete captured response of the Springer Nature Meta API. On a local text route,
  a qualification whose required evidence is binary does not settle a request.
- `status` reports the workspace's current direct deposit as `external_deposit`. At `evaluate`, after a production
  deposit with a DOI, it reports a pending manuscript review as `managed_wait`. For a claim of the opening bundle,
  `round` refuses a new or changed `revised` marker whose `previous` is not the opening claim text
  (`round_claim_revision_mismatch`). The privacy check also matches BOM-free UTF-16 and UTF-32 forms. A missing
  manifest blob whose digest names a valid artifact gives `evidence_manifest_required`. New verification prompts
  state the stance, band and sampling rules.
- The regression ledger has 16 new `real_shape` entries, 11 open entries become `real_shape`, and five entries of
  0.73.6 gain guard tests.

No stored record changes, and no migration is necessary.

## Checks

- The 62 new and changed test modules, with the registry and report-pinning modules, pass on Python 3.12.8 and
  3.9.6 with 1,042 tests each and no skipped test. After the texts were merged, the nine modules that read the
  documents, the skills, the strategies and the version files pass on both Pythons with 188 tests each.
- Compile on both Pythons, `codex/generate.py --check` and `git diff --check` pass. The eleven protected modules and
  the fit orders are unchanged.
- On a copy of the bunkbed record, two interleaved runs of `status` take 240.3 s and 240.0 s of CPU time with 0.74.1
  and 79.9 s and 79.5 s with this release, with the same report. On a copy of the 2402.14112 study,
  `Store.snapshot()` takes 78.2 s and 79.6 s with 0.74.1 and 52.0 s and 52.2 s with this release, with the same
  snapshot.
- On copies of the kmp and bunkbed records, the 1,575 request derivations of the assignments that the restoration
  policy selects give the same requests with the exact size arithmetic as with a full measure of each trial request.
- `status` and `next` of this release and of 0.74.1 on copies of six problem records differ only where the
  per-statement closure intends: on two posted records, the closed chains of native openness reviews lose their
  next actions. The plan-per-head work compared 22 copies with 0.74.0 with the same result, and `record-check` is
  ready on the copies of the graph rigidity, bunkbed and kmp records.
- The texts were written by Fable 5.1. Astra 6 reviewed the first part once at `max`: 12 of its 14 findings are
  applied; two are kept out because the verification rules state the server's verdict contract.
