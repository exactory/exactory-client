# Exactory 0.70.0

This release performs a problem result review or a focused adjudication whose request does not fit one input of a
local route as a staged review: tasks of one frozen epoch, each a fresh call with an empty history, that read the
originals, write grounded cards, assess the cards together, read selected originals again, integrate the findings
and write the review. The harness plans every task and names the next command; the operator runs the commands. An
assessment that cannot be completed, by the reviewer or by the bounded execution, is recorded as a distinct outcome
that occupies its slot. A packet that fits one input and delivers every body is built as before. The release
follows 0.69.11; the notes of 0.69.5 to 0.69.11 describe the citation matching changes of those releases. The work
is recorded in `misc/harness-improvement/20261031190348-part-delivery-quality-equivalence` of the exactory
repository.

## Why

0.69.0 to 0.69.4 deliver an oversized result packet in part turns of one session. A part turn returns one free-text
note per unit, checked for shape only, and the final turn holds the earlier units only through those notes, the
verified excerpts and the earlier packets that the replayed history still carries. On 2026-10-31 the first part
series of the unknotting record delivered about 2.1 million characters of units in three part turns per slot, and
both final reviews were `insufficient` on calibration; the reviewer wrote that its review rested on the frame of the
final turn, its notes and its excerpts. The replayed history grew about 30,000 characters per turn, and the evening's
requests were close to the Claude limit of 1,300,000 characters: 1,299,415 and 1,294,633 for part turn 4 of the two
unknotting slots, 1,289,020 for the slot A final of the KMP record. No measurement compared a review in parts with a
review of the same evidence in one input.

The owner asked for a split that keeps the quality of a single input, with no regression, at scale and in every case:
chunked reading of the originals, critical statements kept verbatim, structured summaries grounded in verified
excerpts, a stage that assesses combinations of evidence, further calls that read the originals of the flagged
combinations, and a final turn that can return to any original. GPT 6 Astra at effort xhigh designed the mechanism in
four drafts and two independent reviews; the supervisor's decisions are section 18 of the design. The
[decision guide](../research-decisions.md#staged-problem-reviews) states the contract, and the
[CLI reference](../research-cli.md#staged-problem-reviews) the commands, the records and the codes.

## What changed

- Entry. A problem `result` or `adjudicator` assignment on `claude_cli_v1`, `codex_cli_v1`, `codex_cli_framed_v1` or
  `codex_cli_visual_v1` stays ordinary when the request that the transport builds fits the route limit and delivers
  every item of the packet and every artifact body of its `evidence` list (on the visual route, also every page image
  of its native sources); the packet, the prompt and the request are then built as before, byte for byte. Otherwise,
  also below the limit when the fitted request withholds a body, the assignment becomes the controller of a staged
  review (`staged_review: {protocol: "exactory-review-staged-v2", kind: "controller", epoch_id, task_ids}`). An
  initial assignment, a refresh, a continuation, a context repair and a focused adjudication enter this way; a
  `source_delivery` continuation and a turn that continues a recorded 0.69.x part series with the same evidence
  digest do not.
- The epoch. `review-assignment` freezes `review_epoch/<id>` in the same write: the subject, the evidence currency,
  the role, the slot, the reviewer, the route and the author; a manifest of every source as text with its length and
  SHA-256, every occurrence of the evidence lists with the bodies it owns, every claim of the frame with its context,
  the assurance dependencies and the inherited material objections; the material objections with their evidence
  resolved; the permitted value of every decision; and the budgets (10,000 calls, one retry per task, two rereads of
  an unchanged question, 10,000 subdivisions, two epochs per slot and evidence currency, 86,400 seconds). It installs
  the slot head `review_slot_head/<slot id>` as `pending`, so an earlier review of the slot leaves the current pair
  before any call, and plans the frame tasks in groups of the largest size whose joint measurement fits the route.
- The tasks. Seven task types, `frame`, `read`, `combine`, `original_check`, `integrate`, `answer` and `final`, each
  with an explicit scope (`joint_original`, `segmented_original`, `original_segment`, `joint_card`, `segmented_card`,
  `card_segment` or `unresolved`), frozen inputs, claims with their context roots, dependencies and a measurement in
  three dimensions: input characters against the route limit, projected output tokens with a margin against the
  route's `max_output_tokens`, and projected seconds against its timeout. A task's prompt is the role's current
  template text followed by the staged texts of its type under the id `exactory-review-staged-v2/<role>/<type>`; its
  packet carries the originals as exact text spans, the accepted records of its dependencies, the adverse records of
  refused outputs, its output budget as `staged_review.output_tokens` (the route's `max_output_tokens` minus the
  planner's margin), and nothing replayed.
- The planner (`research_harness/review_staged_protocol.py`, with `review_staged_followups.py` for rereads and page
  plans and `review_staged_binary.py` for native originals). `review-assignment {controller_assignment_id}` plans the
  ready frontier of the epoch from the accepted outputs in one store transaction: the remaining read, combine,
  original check, integration and final tasks of the core planner, the rereads that pending `retrievals` request
  under a shared budget, the answer pages of a composition plan, and the subdivision of a task whose two attempts
  were refused. It freezes one ready task, records its child assignment itself and returns the exact `review-run`
  payload; after the final it returns the `value-review` or `review-adjudication` payload. The sizing profiles are
  bootstrap estimates (`unqualified-planning-v1`) that the observed usage of each route raises.
- The operator's commands. `review-assignment` records the controller and returns the planning command; the operator
  alternates `review-assignment {controller_assignment_id}` and `review-run {assignment_id, epoch_id, task_id}`
  until `next` is the recording payload. `action: "accept"` resumes an acceptance and `action: "reconcile"`
  reconciles a worker, neither with a reviewer call. `next` never repeats an exhausted task, and no step asks the
  operator to build a task envelope.
- Capacity. One reviewer call at a time per store by default, on the four local adapters. Every covered call passes
  one admission in the transaction that records its start: a staged task's reservation, an ordinary `review-run`, a
  `review-probe` and a `review-output-correction`. `review-assignment` with `scheduler: {store_limit,
  route_limits}` records a higher capacity (`review_capacity_policy/store`); `review_capacity_wait` names the
  reservations to reconcile.
- Capture, acceptance and refusal. `review-run` reserves the task, launches an owned worker, waits for its capture
  and accepts it. The worker commits the observation through the store (`review_observation/<reservation id>`);
  that commit is the capture, and no file outside the store is an observation. The attempt and its capture binding
  are recorded before acceptance. Acceptance (`review_task_acceptance/<reservation id>`) authenticates the capture
  against the frozen request, binds every quotation to its source span, checks the output against the task's shape,
  records the task's actual input and indexes the acceptance by task, so a later task reads exactly the dependencies
  it names and a sibling's commit does not invalidate a prepared one. An output that fails its check is refused
  (`review_task_refusal/<reservation id>`) and keeps its bytes; the task gets one retry with the same request, and
  after a second refusal the planner subdivides it (`review_task_repair/<task id>`) or, for a final task whose
  refused output is a complete review, plans answer pages from that output. A worker that dies before the capture
  leaves an unknown outcome, one retry, then `review_task_exhausted`. A reservation is released only after confirmed
  termination of the worker and its process group. Capture and acceptance commit through task-scoped writes
  (`Store.mutate_scoped`, `prepared_task_mutation`).
- The resource ledger. `review_resource_ledger/<epoch id>` is charged in the transaction that reserves each task
  (attempts, retries, reread rounds, subdivisions, epochs, elapsed seconds). A reservation over a budget writes
  `review_execution_exhaustion/<id>` and is refused, and the harness records an execution-origin
  `assessment_incomplete` with the recovery work.
- Raw observation and effective result. `assignment_state` exposes `raw_output` and `raw_output_digest`, the exact
  object a call returned, and `effective`, the scientific result assembled from the accepted tasks, of kind
  `ordinary`, `paged`, `incomplete` or `pending`; `read_raw_observation` returns the raw observation of an attempt.
  Authentication, refusal classification and output correction read the raw observation; recording, independence,
  coverage, objections, context repair and export read the effective result. `value-review` and
  `review-adjudication` write `effective_binding` on the record and move the slot head to `complete`. A paged final
  is answer pages and a terminal bound under a composition root (`review_composition.py`): one owner per field, lists
  by frozen ordinals, string segments joined in order, no last-write-wins, and a terminal that authorizes the exact
  root and chooses only the scalar values the record permits.
- Grounding. Every conclusion and condition is resolved from its own grounds and dependencies; a condition the
  reviewer marks unsatisfied blocks the assurances that depend on it; a quotation that is empty after normalization
  is refused as a ground. A frame task's claims, conditions, dependencies and unresolved items enter a versioned
  context graph; a task planned under an older claim context is superseded and leaves the frontier, and its child
  is refused. An answer page's grounds are checked against the spans its task received and the exact accepted,
  resolved records its packet carried, at the acceptance of the answer task, again at composition and when the
  task graph is collected.
- Incomplete assessments. One code, `assessment_incomplete`, for every incomplete assessment. A final task that
  cannot complete a required assessment returns `assessment_incomplete {unresolved_scope, recovery_work}`, and the
  harness records the same outcome itself when the execution is exhausted: attempts, calls, subdivisions, rereads or
  elapsed time at their budget, an unknown worker outcome, a binary original that the route cannot deliver, or a
  frame that no call can carry. The record `assessment-incomplete:<digest>` carries its origin, its binding, its
  roots, its resource ledger and its unresolved entries; the slot head moves to `incomplete`, the earlier adverse
  findings stay in force, and the gate lists `assessment_incomplete` with the recovery work instead of asking for
  another reviewer. A successor epoch is opened with `staged_upgrade` and is bounded to two epochs per slot and
  evidence currency.
- The post gate. `assess_problem_reviews` reads the slot outcomes: pending and incomplete slots are obligations in
  their own slots, only completed slots form the pair, and the result coverage check reads the accepted task graph.
  Every staged slot also lists `review_quality_measurement_pending`, so the gate is not ready for a staged review in
  this release. The gate report carries `slot_outcomes`, `status --summary` carries `review_slots`, and in a record
  that holds an epoch the obligations of a staged review are ordered before the scientific shortfalls; a record
  without an epoch keeps the obligation order of 0.69.4.
- Focused adjudication. A staged adjudicator's epoch freezes the review pair and the objection version;
  `review-adjudication` records the controller's effective result, refuses a changed pair or version
  (`review_adjudication_mismatch`) and an incomplete graph (`review_task_pending`), and keeps the objection open
  when the adjudication is incomplete. A staged source review of the pair is read through its `effective_binding`.
- Recorded sessions and lineage. `review-assignment` with `context: {staged_upgrade: {assignment_id}}` opens a
  staged review in the slot of a recorded result or adjudicator assignment, or of the controller of an incomplete
  epoch. The successor inherits the predecessor's recorded reviews and material objections, the adverse findings of
  its accepted tasks and the adverse records of its refused outputs, and records its own review without a collision
  with the predecessor's. Every consumer reads the scientific predecessor from `prior_assignment_id`,
  `context_repair.assignment_id` or `staged_upgrade.assignment_id`.
- Context repair. A repair of a staged `result` or `adjudicator` slot waits for pending workers
  (`review_context_repair_pending`), authenticates the staged task history against its frozen requests, carries the
  predecessor's raw observations, intermediate findings and effective binding, reads its pending source requests with
  the epoch's manifest, keeps an adjudicator's objection and review pair, and no longer needs a recorded final for a
  part turn of a 0.69.x series.
- Views and exports. `intermediate/reviews/staged/<kind>/` holds the staged record kinds, `final/reviews/incomplete/`
  the incomplete outcomes, and a controller's turn writes its effective output beside its raw turns. The problem
  export adds `reviews.json` when a review of the decision is staged. The renderer waits at most one second for its
  lock and otherwise leaves a dirty marker and the advisory reason `render_deferred`. `review-run` refuses a payload
  that is not a JSON object with a structured `invalid_input`.

## What stays as it was

- A packet whose fitted request fits and delivers every item and every body is built, measured and recorded exactly
  as before, with the same prompt and request bytes. An ordinary post without an epoch reports the same first
  obligation as 0.69.4.
- No module that a route pins by its bytes changed: `review_transport.py`, `review_claude_transport.py`,
  `review_codex_transport.py`, `review_codex_framed_transport.py`, `review_codex_visual_transport.py`,
  `review_verbatim_text.py`, `review_exact_text.py`, `review_exact_text_v2.py`, `review_exact_text_cost_v1.py`,
  `review_visual_inputs.py` and `review_pdf_pages.py` are as in 0.69.11. Every recorded route stays valid and needs
  no new probe, and the reduction orders do not change.
- The legacy part prompts, the part response shape, the part planner and the series coverage check are unchanged.
  `Store.mutate`, `prepared_mutation` and the full-history validation of the store are unchanged.
- The Responses route, bar and slate reviews, study, manuscript and verification reviews and the native math reviews
  are not staged.

## Existing records

- A recorded 0.69.0 to 0.69.4 part or final turn authenticates as recorded, and a turn that continues such a series
  with the same evidence digest, before the result review of its final turn is recorded, continues it in parts under
  the recorded contract. A continuation whose evidence digest changed and a refresh of a series (a new assignment
  after the recorded review) start new work under the 0.70.0 entry rule: ordinary when the request fits and delivers
  every mandatory body, staged otherwise, and never part turn 1. An explicit `staged_upgrade` moves a recorded slot
  to a staged review at once.
- Installation alone changes neither active record. The unknotting and KMP records run their result reviews on 0.69.2
  to 0.69.4; the owner decides whether they are reviewed again in the staged mode.
- The store is append-only and needs no migration. The new record kinds (`review_epoch`, `review_task`,
  `review_execution_plan`, `review_task_execution`, `review_reservation`, `review_pool_membership`,
  `review_observation`, `review_task_acceptance`, `review_task_acceptance_index`, `review_epoch_discoveries`,
  `review_capacity_policy`, `review_slot_head`, `review_task_dispatch`, `review_capture_attempt`,
  `review_task_refusal`, `review_task_repair`, `review_composition_plan`, `review_resource_ledger`,
  `review_execution_exhaustion` and `assessment_incomplete`) appear only in a record that opens a staged review. A
  resource ledger for an epoch written before this record is reconstructed once from its reservations.

## Codes

  `review_worker_reconciliation_required`, `review_observation_acceptance_pending`,
  `review_task_dependency_changed`, `review_epoch_stale`, `review_composition_pending`,
  `review_composition_conflict`, `review_assurance_dependency_pending`, `assessment_incomplete` and
  `review_quality_measurement_pending`.
- New refusals: `review_task_exhausted`, `review_task_failed`, `review_instrumented_capture_required`,
  `review_context_repair_pending`, `review_staged_binary_unsupported`, `review_route_unsupported` and
  `invalid_review_staged`; `review_resampling_forbidden` also refuses a second child of one task and a rerun under
  another attempt id of a captured reservation, and `review_invocation_pending` also refuses a retry while a task's
  reservation is unreleased.
- `review_staged_scale_unsupported` refuses staged entry, upgrade or re-freezing when a required all-claim envelope
  or the smallest full-card output exceeds the route's input limit or visible output allowance.
  Its details report the claim and occurrence counts, envelope characters, input limit, estimated card output tokens
  and output allowance; no epoch, task or slot change is written.
- Completion issues inside a report: `review_terminal_pending`, `review_finding_answer_pending`,
  `review_original_check_pending`, `review_source_coverage_pending`, `review_card_pending`,
  `review_claim_cell_pending`, `review_claim_unresolved`, `review_combination_pending`, `review_integration_pending`,
  `review_integration_unresolved` and `review_retrieval_pending`.
- Unchanged in meaning: `review_input_too_large`, `problem_evidence_part_pending`, `problem_evidence_unassessed`,
  `review_evidence_undelivered`, `review_invocation_unverified`, `review_context_contaminated`.

## Known limits

- Staged reviews still require one full card to judge every claim and later tasks to carry every claim identity and
  context root, so some large records cannot complete under these task contracts.
  A preflight refuses that scale before freezing an epoch, including the synthetic 15,389-claim, 106-occurrence case;
  paged logical cards and a different representation for this scale are planned for the next release.
  A repair may also be refused when its retained history expands the frozen claim inventory beyond these budgets.
  A slot whose staged epoch would exceed the route budgets keeps the 0.69.x part delivery on automatic entry.
  An explicit `staged_upgrade` of such a slot is refused with `review_staged_scale_unsupported`.
  Completion diagnostics now use a versioned bounded report with exact counts and sorted-set digests, at most twenty
  examples per issue code, and an exact detail iterator over the authenticated selection.
- The quality-equivalence measurement has not run. The release claims no equal quality between a staged review and a
  review of the same evidence in one input. The staged mode decides no posting: every staged slot lists
  `review_quality_measurement_pending`, and no author flag lifts it, until an authenticated passing study (option A,
  then option C of the design: 150 independent held-out fixtures, zero composite regressions, one-sided 95% upper
  bound 1.98%) binds the protocol and the reviewer configuration. The issuer of that qualification record is not in
  this release. The owner's confirmation of the measurement was pending when this note was written.
- Full-history storage validation is unchanged: every authoritative read still validates the whole history, measured
  at 2.041 seconds for a copy of the unknotting record and 1.707 for a copy of the KMP record. No history-independent
  command latency is claimed.
- The sizing profiles are bootstrap estimates under the calibration version `unqualified-planning-v1`, raised by the
  observed usage of each route; they qualify nothing. The turn counts and wall times of the design (18 to 56 calls
  per slot, 7.63 serial hours at the Opus max mean) are projections under stated assumptions, not measurements of
  this release.
- The ordinary path was profiled on the 55 tests of `test_research_problem_objections` against the archived 0.69.4
  source: the profile of this release has no call into the staged, execution or composition modules, and the counts
  of assignment states, snapshots and validations equal the archived ones, but the total of primitive calls is 5.49%
  above 0.69.4 and the wall times were taken under a shared machine load of 18 to 47 on 12 CPUs. No elapsed-time
  parity with 0.69.4 is claimed; a controlled benchmark remains to be run.
- A binary original is delivered only on `codex_cli_visual_v1`, as every page of the PDF under the route's native
  page contract; on the other local routes a task that needs it ends as an execution-origin incomplete outcome.
