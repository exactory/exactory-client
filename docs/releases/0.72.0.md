# Exactory 0.72.0

This release delivers a review whose input does not fit one route input as a series of fresh-input turns, under the
protocol `exactory-review-parts-v2` ("parts v2" below). A unit is one splittable packet entry together with its
attached artifact contents. A card is the structured reviewer record returned for a unit; passage units share their
reading's card. A target is a check, rubric item, objection or other question that the final review must decide. An
adapter is a module that defines a role's splittable packet collections, review targets, citation forms and final
validator. A series has read, combine, check and final turns. Each turn is a fresh input with no transport history.
The first turn freezes an append-only inventory of the review's units. Read turns return cards whose statements the
harness verifies against the delivered text and keeps with their locations. Combine turns name the joint questions
and the exact originals to read. Check turns answer them. The final turn writes the ordinary review of its role, or
requests originals, subject to state budgets and a fit check before each assignment. This release provides adapters
for problem results, focused problem adjudications, context repairs, study verification, study readiness and native
mathematics. The release also bounds the retained reviewer excerpt state (`exactory-review-excerpts-v3`). It follows
0.71.8. The work is recorded in `misc/harness-improvement/20261103013817-release-0-72-0-oversized-reviews-in-fresh-parts`
of the exactory repository.

## Why

Measured on 2026-11-03 on copies of the record stores and in the stores of idle records, on the code of 0.71.3:

- Six focused adjudications were refused with `review_input_too_large`: four in the Sidorenko record
  (`assignment-adjudication-post-activity`, `assignment-adjudication-small-absent` twice,
  `assignment-adjudication-small-missing`) and two in the quantum codes record
  (`assignment-adjudication-primary-sources`, `assignment-adjudication-weight-window`). Focused adjudications kept
  their ordinary packets whatever their size, so the ordinary `review-run` path could not produce dispositions for
  these assignments. An explicit `staged_upgrade` remained available.
- A turn of a 0.69.x part series replayed its whole history. In series 4 of the random cokernel record the replayed
  history of turns 1 to 8 measured 385,563 to 545,411 characters; in series 4 of the quantum codes record, 402,278 to
  514,046. The current packet of such a turn measured 700,000 to 1,070,000 characters.
- The final turn of such a series carried every objection's evidence at once: 904,820 characters in slot A of the
  quantum codes record (`material_objections` 502,196 characters in 14 objections, the largest 171,649) and 773,411
  in slot A of the random cokernel record (`material_objections` 399,885). The route limits are 1,300,000 characters
  on the Claude route and 1,048,576 on the Codex routes.
- The measured high-effort turns approached or exceeded the output cap. Part turns of the unknotting record at effort
  max returned 102,927 to 123,721 output tokens against a cap of 128,000. The bar repairs of the KMP and unknotting records ended over the
  cap, at 187,401 to 217,901 tokens, as `review_incomplete`.

Two conclusions follow. The replayed history and the objection evidence take most of a part turn's input, not the
problem evidence frame. A read turn must be sized against the output cap as well as the input limit. The owner
approved the direction on 2026-10-31: read the originals in chunks, keep the critical statements verbatim, ground the
summaries in verified statements, combine over the compressed material, read the originals of the selected
combinations, and let the final turn return to any original. This release implements that direction. It replaces the
staged v3 plan of the part-delivery record (section 19 of its design). It does not build on the 0.70.0 staged epochs,
which no record has completed.

## What changed

### Reviews in fresh parts

- Entry. On a text route with a local input limit (`claude_cli_v1`, `codex_cli_v1`, `codex_cli_framed_v1`), and for
  a role and packet that have an adapter, `review-assignment` builds the ordinary request first. For new work
  outside an existing series, a request that fits and carries every evidence body stays ordinary and is recorded as
  before, byte for byte, unless a repair rule requires parts: an admitted adjudicator repair uses parts whatever its
  size, and an earlier overflow of the same repair binding also requires parts. A request that does not fit, or
  whose fitted request withholds a body, starts a parts v2 series. A recorded 0.69.x part series continues under its
  own protocol until its final. After a refresh of its recorded final, or a changed evidence digest, the request is
  measured again under these rules. A staged review still starts only through an explicit `staged_upgrade`. On a
  route without a local input limit, on the visual route, for a `complete-visible-v1` delivery and for a native
  review with an openness minimum, nothing changes.
- Fresh inputs. The request of every turn carries no replayed conversation: its `history` is `[]`. What a later turn
  needs from earlier turns travels inside its packet as records that the harness builds from the authenticated outputs
  of those turns (`review_parts_state`). The bar session of a result review is not replayed; the reviewer's own bar
  and both bars stay in the frame, as in every result packet.
- Units, frame and inventory. The adapter declares the splittable collections of its role's packet. A unit is one
  entry of such a collection with the artifact bodies it owns; equal items at several places are one unit with
  several occurrences, and each occurrence stays an obligation. The frame is the packet with every unit removed, and
  every turn carries it. The first turn stores the inventory as an artifact: for each unit its handle, item digest,
  item characters, occurrences, owned bodies (SHA-256, kind, characters) and `citations`, the exact references by
  which the role's validator accepts a citation of the unit's content. It also stores the logical packet, the
  complete packet before projection. Every turn binds both digests. The inventory is append-only: a unit that appears
  later, for example a new `version_evidence` entry that the other slot's final added to an objection, gets its own
  read turn; a unit that disappears ends the series.
- Segments. A text body, or an item whose canonical JSON does not fit one read turn, is delivered in exact segments at
  Unicode code point offsets `[start, end)`. A segment ends at a paragraph or line boundary in the last quarter of
  its allowed span when one is available, and at the size limit otherwise. The body counts as delivered when the
  segments of the series cover it. A body whose encoding is not UTF-8 is delivered whole and never segmented; a
  unit whose whole non-text body does not fit one read turn is refused before the first turn with
  `review_parts_inventory_too_large`. The frame is not segmented: a frame that leaves no room under the route limit
  is refused before the first turn with `review_parts_frame_too_large` and the sizes of its ten largest fields.
- Turns. A read turn delivers units, each at its first occurrence, with their bodies or segments, and the list of
  targets of the role (for a problem result: the five problem checks, every rubric item and every material and
  historical objection; for an adjudication: the objection's premise, the statement, the resolution criteria, each
  value paragraph and, for a rubric objection, the item; for a verification: the three checks; for a readiness
  review: the six readiness checks). The reviewer returns one card per delivered unit (`summary`, `bearing` on
  targets with the relation `supports`, `qualifies`, `contradicts` or `context`, `statements`, `depends_on`,
  `questions`) and `unborne`, the targets that no card of the turn bears on. A combine turn reads the cards (all of
  them, or two blocks when they do not fit one input) and returns the combinations that need a joint reading of
  originals, each with the exact originals to read, and `rest`, why the other cards need no joint reading. A check
  turn carries the combinations with the cards of their units and the originals they named, and returns one finding
  per combination (`effect` one of `supports`, `qualifies`, `contradicts`, `unresolved`). The final turn carries the
  frame, every card, combination and finding, and a fill of originals in its spare room. It returns the role's
  ordinary output, or a request (`{"kind": "requests", "unit_requests": [...], "source_requests": [...]}`) that a
  further final turn answers with the requested originals first. The planner chooses a read turn while any unit
  lacks complete item or body delivery. It chooses combine turns until the required card and block pairs have been
  read together. It then chooses check turns while combinations lack findings, and a final turn otherwise. A series
  with at most one card needs no combine turn, and a series without combinations needs no check turn.
- Verified statements. Every statement of a card or a finding is checked against the text that the turn delivered
  for its unit, with the normalization of the excerpt rules (Unicode compatibility normalization, NFKC, applied to
  each character, and white space collapsed). Only verified statements are carried. Each keeps `at`: the unit, the
  body's SHA-256 or `item: true`, and the Unicode code point offsets `[start, end)` in the whole text; they locate the
  first matching occurrence in the delivered text. The carried card records how many statements were not verified. An unverified
  statement does not refuse the output. Combination ids are assigned by the harness (`c<turn>-<n>`).
- Sizes. Every planned packet is measured as its exact rendered request. The read allowance starts at 600,000 unit
  characters and follows the observed output of each read turn: half after a turn whose output tokens reached 75
  percent of the route cap, one and a half times after a turn at 35 percent or less, unchanged otherwise. When a
  failed read turn qualifies for replacement after an output-cap failure or `review_incomplete`, the replacement
  starts with half the failed turn's measured `unit_characters`. The state that the final carries must fit the room
  that the final frame leaves: cards at most half of it, combinations a tenth, findings a fifth, the rest the fill.
  The planner checks each proposed turn against these budgets before it records the assignment, so a series can be
  refused at entry or during continuation. The cards of a read turn get at most 80,000
  characters and at least 4,000, in proportion to the turn's share of the uncarded unit characters; a combine or check
  output and the request form of a final get at most 60,000 characters; the role's ordinary output in a final has no
  parts allowance. Statements count 40,000 characters per turn and 6,000 per statement. Every read turn delivers
  something that no earlier turn delivered. A final request must name at least one original or source that the final
  it answers did not carry, and the final turns of a series carry at most twice the inventory's characters of
  originals.
- Verification. `verify_series` authenticates every turn (an instrumented, completed and ready attempt, its actual
  request read back with the frozen packet, and an output under the contract of its kind) and reports whether the
  series is complete: every occurrence of every unit credited by a read turn, one card per delivered unit with every
  target borne or `unborne`, every card read by a combine turn unless the series has at most one card, every pair of
  blocks met once, every combination with a finding, and an observed final with the role's ordinary output. One reader, `read_series_delivery`, builds
  what a series delivered (complete attachments, and segments with their exact offsets), so a body delivered in three
  segments is citable and counts for coverage. The series delivery replaces the duplicate attempt authentication of
  the citation reader that 0.71.0 listed as cleanup D2-2.
- Series identity. A series is valid when its turns share role, route, slot, session, reviewer, author, subject and
  the adapter's series key, and each turn's `prior_assignment_id` names the turn before it. The keys: problem result,
  the problem evidence digest and the digest of the bars; focused adjudication, the problem evidence digest, the
  objection's material version and the digest of the current-source requirements; repair, the role's key and the
  repair binding; verification and native reviews, the digest of the frame; readiness, the digest of the frame
  without its transport envelope. A changed series key or a removed inventory unit ends the series. The next request
  is measured again and starts another parts series only when parts are still required. The cards of the ended
  series are not carried.
- Prompts. The rendered prompt of a turn is the role's base prompt followed by the parts v2 texts of its kind and
  adapter, under the template id `exactory-review-parts-v2/<adapter>/<kind>` (`final-answer` for a final that answers
  a request), with the base prompt's descriptor under `parameters.base_prompt` and the binding under
  `parameters.review_parts`. Every existing template id, prompt text and hash stays as it is.

### Roles

- Problem result (`problem_result`). Units: the six split lists of `problem_evidence` of the 0.69.x series
  (`openness_searches`, `assessment_evidence`, `readings`, `passages`, `computations`, `formal_checks`), a passage
  carded under its reading, and every `version_evidence` entry of the material objections and of the objections of
  the historical reviews. The post gate credits a review whose assignment is a parts v2 final only when the series is
  complete and its read turns cover every expected item and body of the current evidence.
- Focused problem adjudication (`problem_adjudicator`). Units: the problem evidence lists, the original evidence, the
  version evidence and the current sources with their bodies. An intermediate turn is checked only for what it
  declares it delivers; the completed series is checked against the stored logical packet with the series' delivery,
  so every manifest, projection, inspected-unit, version and descriptor comparison stays exact. A disposition binds
  only from the final of a complete series: `review-adjudication` refuses every other turn with
  `review_parts_pending`. The current adjudicator prompt check accepts the parts final variants, so a parts v2 final
  can return `not_binding`, and a ready parts v2 adjudication on unchanged evidence is not sampled again. A focused
  refresh starts a new series with the same adjudicator, route and author. A focused assignment recorded before
  0.72.0 that never ran, and whose frozen request fails the size preflight, can be replaced by a new assignment with
  the same adjudicator, route, author, `review_ids`, objection and correction; that assignment enters parts v2, and
  the old record stays.
- Context repair (`context_repair`). On a text route with a local input limit, a repair of a bar, slate or result
  review runs as a parts v2 series when its ordinary repair request does not fit, or when an earlier attempt with the
  same role, dossier, slot and repair predecessor ended over the output cap; a repair whose request fits stays
  ordinary (or a staged controller, for a staged predecessor). A repair of an adjudication, which is admitted for a
  staged predecessor and from this release for a parts v2 predecessor, runs as a parts v2 series on such a route
  whatever its size. The adapter wraps the role adapter where the role has one: the units are the role's units of
  the current packet and the scientific history (each ancestor's observed or intermediate output, its frame and its
  packet's units); for a bar or slate repair the history units are the only units. The frame holds the repair
  binding and every inherited final with its exact payload and objection identities. `prepare_repair` accepts a parts
  v2 predecessor and classifies every nonterminal parts output as intermediate; `validate_reassessment` checks the
  reassessment against the series' delivery. The base prompt of an adjudicator repair is the ordinary problem
  adjudicator prompt, and the final text of the repair states the repair rules.
- Study verification (`study_verification`) and readiness (`study_readiness`). A verification review (role
  `verification`) whose packet does not fit runs as a series over the entries of `paper.sources.work`,
  `paper.sources.reading`, `paper.sources.source_bundle` and `paper.sources.source`. `require_verdict_assignment`
  accepts only the final of a complete series and reads the task digest from the series frame. A readiness review
  (role `result` with a strategic dossier) runs as a series over the entries of `readiness.inputs` and
  `readiness.execution_observations`, with the selected candidate and the synthesis kept in the frame. The readiness,
  direction and scoped-support readers credit only the delivery of a complete authenticated series. A candidate
  evidence reference is covered when the final's support evidence cites it, or when the complete series carries an
  authenticated card of a unit whose inventory `citations` include that reference and whose bearing names one of the
  six readiness targets. A verification turn continues only its own series with the same route, reviewer, author and
  task; a readiness continuation rebuilds the current readiness, dossier and support contract. No study store holds a
  review assignment yet, so these adapters are tested on synthetic fixtures only.
- Native mathematics (`native_math`). A `native_math` review whose packet does not fit, and to which no openness
  reading chain applies, runs as a series over the entries of the dependency closure of the subject and the list
  collections of its scientific context; a review with an openness minimum keeps its reading chain. The reviewer's
  attestation names the series' first turn, as it names the first turn of a reading chain. The math-solver harness
  accepts the final of a complete series as it accepts a reading chain's final, records the series receipts under
  `reading_turns` with empty unit lists, and requires the supplied native packet to equal the series' logical packet
  (`review_packet_mismatch` otherwise).

### Views and commands

- The assignment result of a parts v2 turn holds `review_parts_plan`: `kind`, `turn`, `units_carded`,
  `units_remaining`, `unit_allowance`, `card_allowance` and `remaining_turns` per kind, each marked `estimate` and
  computed from the series' own turns only. Before `review-run`, the first step that calls a model, the operator
  reads `review_parts_plan` in the assignment result, and the allowances, estimates, progress and next action in
  `status` under `review.assignments.<id>.review_parts`.
- `status` reports every parts turn under `review.assignments.<id>.review_parts`: the adapter, the series id, the
  kind and the turn, the units carded and remaining, the allowances and the estimates, whether the series is
  complete, its issues (a count and at most 20 examples), `blocked` with a code when the turn cannot continue, and
  `next`, the next action as `{kind, workflow, scope}`: `review-assignment` with the last turn as
  `prior_assignment_id`, or `review-run` of the recorded turn. An action is not a payload: the operator builds the
  command's complete payload from it (a new id, the same role, dossier, route, reviewer and author, and
  `prior_assignment_id` inside `context`, with `native_packet` or `verification_task_id` there when the role needs
  it). `status --summary` adds `review_parts: {count, examples, omitted}` with at most 20 series. A problem slot with
  an incomplete series lists the obligation `review_parts_pending` with the same fields and the next action; it ranks
  with the other review task obligations, and `next` names the step.
- `export --kind outputs` places the outputs of read, combine and check turns, and the request form of a final,
  under `intermediate/reviews/<role>/<series assignment id>/<NN>-<assignment id>.<run id>.json`, and the selected
  final output of a complete series under `final/reviews/<role>/<series assignment id>/`: an ordinary final as
  `<NN>-<assignment id>.<run id>.json`, a corrected final as `<NN>-<assignment id>.<correction id>.corrected.json`;
  the run's `output_path` or the correction's `corrected_output_path` gives the exact location. Each turn in
  `series.json` has a `review_parts` progress object (`protocol`, `adapter`, `series_assignment_id`, `kind`, `turn`,
  the allowances, the estimates, the evaluated progress and the next actions), and the output index entries carry
  `series_assignment_id`, `turn`, `adapter` and `review_parts_kind`.
- `review-assignment --check` keeps its documented contract: a preparation that needs the reviewer CLI, which every
  parts v2 route does, is refused with `check_unsupported` and writes nothing.
- When an authenticated run completes, `review-run` checks read, combine and check outputs and the request form of a
  final under `review.shape`, whatever the role. A contract failure is refused with `invalid_value_review`; a request
  that makes no progress with `review_parts_request_no_progress`. The existing refusal rules apply: one retry with
  the same request, and `review_slot_exhausted` after two refused outputs.

### Bounded excerpt state

New reviewer excerpt sessions and new openness reading records use the session protocol
`exactory-review-excerpts-v3`. An unmatched excerpt keeps the SHA-256, the length and the first 200 characters of its
text instead of the whole text. A `purpose` longer than 500 characters keeps its first 500 characters with the
SHA-256 and the length of the whole. Excerpts with the same source binding, text digest and status are kept once,
and a verified duplicate does not charge the allowance. An undelivered passage answer keeps the anchor's first 200
characters with the SHA-256 and the length of the whole; `not_found` and `over_allowance` settle the request, and
when capacity defers the reply, the pending request keeps the complete anchor and a later continuation answers that
original request. The excerpt prompt texts and the session allowances are unchanged. Recorded v1 excerpt sessions
continue under v2, recorded v2 sessions stay v2, and existing openness reading records keep their recorded protocol.
Parts entry creates a v3 excerpt session only when the packet has no excerpt session; otherwise the series keeps the
inherited session protocol.

## What stays as it was

- The eleven modules that a route pins by their bytes are unchanged against 0.71.3 (`1921c9e`):
  `review_transport.py`, `review_claude_transport.py`, `review_codex_transport.py`,
  `review_codex_framed_transport.py`, `review_codex_visual_transport.py`, `review_verbatim_text.py`,
  `review_exact_text.py`, `review_exact_text_v2.py`, `review_exact_text_cost_v1.py`, `review_visual_inputs.py` and
  `review_pdf_pages.py`. `FIT_ORDER` and `EARLIER_FIT_ORDERS` are unchanged.
- Every recorded assignment, attempt, prompt, packet and request keeps authenticating and rebuilds byte for byte. A
  recorded 0.69.x part series continues under `exactory-problem-evidence-parts-v1` while its previous turn is a v1
  turn with the same evidence digest and no recorded final. Frozen 0.70.0 staged epochs continue under their frozen
  contracts, and an explicit `staged_upgrade` still enters staged v2. Recorded openness reading chains continue under
  their protocol. Recorded excerpt sessions (`exactory-review-excerpts-v1` and `-v2`) continue under v2 as before.
- The v1 part planner, the staged v2 contracts, the ordinary source continuations and their replay, the openness
  reading chain and the dispatch preflight of an ordinary assignment are unchanged. `bound_history` (0.69.20) applies
  only to v1 continuations. The `staged_entry` parameter of the v1 planner is removed; the 0.70.0 compatibility
  fixtures rebuild without it.
- No store migration. The new records are ordinary assignments with `review_parts` and `review_parts_plan`. The
  series artifacts hold the inventories, the logical packets and the delivered text segments; a repair series also
  stores the historical frames and outputs as artifacts.

## Existing records

- New eligible oversized work enters parts v2; the six refused adjudications above are such a case. An unfinished v1
  series with unchanged evidence continues under v1. After a refresh or an evidence change, the request is measured
  again under the current entry rules.
- A record in the middle of a v1 series finishes it under v1. A record with a staged epoch keeps it, and its slot
  still lists `review_quality_measurement_pending`.
- A session that continues after the upgrade keeps the retention protocol of its recorded excerpt entries. Only a new
  session, and a new openness reading record, starts under v3.

## Codes

- New refusals: `review_parts_frame_too_large` (the frame leaves no room under the route limit; details
  `frame_characters`, `input_limit_characters`, `largest_fields`); `review_parts_inventory_too_large` (the floors of
  the planned read turns exceed the card budget, a non-text body cannot fit one read turn whole, the remaining card
  budget is below its floor of 4,000 characters, the findings budget is below 2,000 characters per pending
  combination, or a combination cannot fit its promised check room); `review_parts_final_too_large` (the final frame
  cannot hold the minimum cards, two card blocks or the combination blocks do not fit the route, or the final frame
  and its state do not fit); `review_parts_no_progress` (the next read turn adds no item or segment);
  `review_parts_request_no_progress` (a final request output requests nothing absent from the answered final, or
  includes unit requests after the final-original budget of the series is exhausted; at planning, no requested
  original fits the next final; a request larger than the remaining room is answered in segments);
  `review_parts_inventory_changed` (a stored inventory does not match its digest, or the current packet differs from
  the inventory; inside `review-assignment` a changed inventory ends the series and starts a new one);
  `review_parts_delivery_invalid` (a turn's actual request or a stored series artifact does not match the inventory:
  a record was edited or the store is damaged, so stop and report it); `review_parts_incomplete` (the native adapter
  or the math-solver harness met a series without an observed final); and `review_parts_target_label_missing` (a
  problem review target has no label in the installed texts).
- New obligation: `review_parts_pending`, with the adapter, the series id, the kind and the turn, the units carded
  and remaining, `blocked` when the turn cannot continue, and `next`.
- New uses of existing codes: `invalid_value_review` under `review.shape` for a read, combine, check or request
  output that fails its contract, whatever the role (`adjudication.shape` and `verification.parts` name the ordinary
  final-output validation of an adjudicator and a verification series); `review_parts_pending` as the refusal of
  `review-adjudication` on a turn that is not the final of a complete series, and of the study readers for an
  incomplete series; `check_unsupported` for `review-assignment --check` of a parts preparation;
  `review_adjudicator_fixed` for a focused continuation that does not continue the selected parts turn with its
  observed output, or that changes the review pair, the objection or the correction; `invalid_review_context` and
  `review_context_unverified` for a verification continuation that does not continue its own observed turn;
  `review_resampling_forbidden` for a continuation of a verification final; `review_source_continuation_fork` for a
  second live continuation of a verification or native turn; `review_packet_mismatch` in the math-solver harness
  when the supplied native packet differs from the series' logical packet.

## What was measured and what was not

- Six real adjudication plans, on writable copies of the Sidorenko and quantum codes records, without a model call
  (`review-assignment --check` refuses a parts preparation, so the copies were written): all six adjudications that
  0.71.x refused plan as parts v2 series, with an estimate of nine read turns each. Sidorenko: 287 units, 5,050,433
  to 5,086,042 unit characters, frames of 320,061 to 322,020 characters, final frames of 358,057 to 360,143. Quantum
  codes: 144 and 145 units, 5,004,575 and 5,024,571 unit characters, frames of 334,161 and 335,902, final frames of
  368,266 and 370,159.
- No real problem result series was measured on a record copy. On the random cokernel copy the slot's predecessor
  already holds a recorded final, so `review-assignment` refused with `review_resampling_forbidden` before planning,
  as it must. The evidence for the result adapter is the six real adjudication plans and a synthetic result series
  with the complete reviewer texts: 119 units, 2,902,636 unit characters, an initial frame of 457,819 characters and
  a final frame of 458,870, on a route limit of 1,300,000 characters. The planner made five read turns (unit
  characters 600,000 four times and 502,636), one combine turn and one final turn, with a largest header of 24,428
  characters and at most 0.144 seconds of planning per turn (Python 3.9; 0.136 seconds on Python 3.12).
- The quality of parts v2 against a single-input review is not measured. The measurement tooling is in the record
  (`measurement/`): a fixture generator with 12 families and 36 paired cases, a runner that forces parts on fitting
  evidence by patching the input limit in its own process, and a scorer. Its dry run completed all 36 pairs and the
  24 extra ordinary runs with 180 planned parts turns and no model call; the dry run checks the tooling, not the
  model. The paid run waits for the owner's budget. With five parts turns per case, one turn for each ordinary review
  and repeat, and one route probe per case, it uses 276 calls; the actual count depends on continuations and
  failures. Equal review quality stays an expectation. The paid run measures regressions on these synthetic
  fixtures; its automatic scores need blinded human assessment of the reasoning and do not establish general quality
  equivalence.
- No end-to-end parts v2 review with a model call has run on a real record. Posting through a parts v2 series is not
  gated by this measurement, as posting through a 0.69.x series was not.

## Known limits and open items

- The paid quality measurement, above.
- The check before the first turn uses the initial read allowance. When the read allowance adapts downward, the added
  read turns can exhaust the card or findings budget, so a series can be refused during continuation
  (`review_parts_inventory_too_large` or `review_parts_final_too_large` at a later turn). A later release can
  pre-check with the smallest allowance the adaptation can reach.
- The base verification prompt does not state the stance values (`sound`, `not_sound`) or the sampled prediction
  rules; the parts v2 final text of a verification states them. No study store has run a verification review yet,
  so the base prompt stays as it is for a later release.
- The study adapters are tested on synthetic fixtures only: no study or verification store holds a
  `review_assignment` record.
- Items of the earlier records judged not needed now, each with its reason in section 3 of the record's `DESIGN.md`:
  the staged v2 epoch normalization, bounded frontier, narrow polling and sibling dispatch (they serve only staged
  v2, which gets no automatic entry since 0.71.0 and whose slots cannot post); the staged replacement of an oversized
  complete-visible delivery (it needs a page-segmented delivery contract on the visual route, whose transport module
  is pinned, and no store holds such a request); the scientific delivery ceilings of 64 MiB per artifact and 512 MiB
  in total (parts v2 delivers artifacts as units, and no current study exceeds them); incremental output groups and
