# Exactory 0.69.2

This release delivers the owned artifact bodies of a problem evidence unit across consecutive part turns of one
result session. It also leaves delivered objection evidence out of part packets and supplies it in the final turn.
The work follows `DESIGN-T3.md` and its independent review in
`misc/harness-improvement/20261031165300-review-planning-rebuilds-lineage-requests` of the exactory repository.

## Why

Two measured failures on 2026-10-31 prevented result sessions from reaching their final turn:

- On the KMP record at revision 738, part turn 6 was refused in both result slots. Each of four duplicate computation
  items owned six bodies and counted 921,500 characters. The first item with the frame needed about 1,400,000
  characters against the route's 1,300,000 limit; the 800,000 unit cap also refused it. Every body could arrive over
  separate turns, and the post gate already accepted cumulative item and artifact delivery.
- On the unknotting record at revision 845, the frame before part turn 3 of slot B left about 236,000 characters of
  room. Material objections occupied 452,028 characters, including their delivered evidence. The estimated room in
  the remaining turns was about 1,050,000 characters for about 1,660,000 characters of remaining units. Part turns
  give no findings on objections, so repeating their evidence consumed room needed for the units.

## What changed

- The planner counts each unit's item and bodies not yet attached in valid turns. A first unit that cannot fit whole
  travels alone, bypassing the full-unit cap. Its pending bodies are added in owned-artifact order while the fitted
  request still attaches them. Selection happens before transport fitting, including for bodies of 4,096 characters
  or fewer that the transport cannot elide. A pending marker can exceed a tiny body, so the fallback checks later
  body prefixes even when an earlier candidate does not fit.
- Every carried item stays in its turn's receipt. Completion is derived separately: a valid turn must have carried
  the item, and valid turns must cumulatively have attached every artifact it owns. An incomplete carried unit stays
  remaining and travels first in the next turn, with another note required for it. Actual attachments are recorded
  even when a body arrived with the frame before the unit that owns it.
- After evidence construction, the projection replaces each unselected owned body with an empty `omitted` entry,
  matched by its complete artifact reference. An earlier body names its earliest valid turn; a pending body states
  that it arrives later. Duplicate units carry their items without repeating earlier bodies.
- Planning and observation require progress: a part must complete a unit or attach a previously missing owned body.
  A frame and item that cannot fit, or an item whose next pending body cannot fit without any progress, produces
  `review_input_too_large`. The final frame can still be refused if it cannot fit; growing reviewer responses remain
  part of the replay, so no fixed number of turns is promised.
- A part packet omits only `version_evidence` and `version_source_mappings` from each material objection. The final
  packet retains both. The part prompt explains these omissions and the two body-delivery markers with the supplied
  reviewer text.

## Existing records

The earlier part suffix remains available, and authentication compares both complete supported prompt envelopes,
including their hashes and common and excerpt versions. New assignments and continuations use the new suffix. The
final suffix, template id, protocol parameter, part response shape and post gate stay the same. Recorded requests,
packets, prompts and digests are not rewritten. No route needs a new probe. The route-pinned transport and cost
modules and `review_state_evaluation.py` do not change.

## Tests

`tests/test_problem_result_parts_continued.py` covers small-body continuation, duplicate units, equal hashes with
different descriptors, cumulative post coverage, objection evidence projection, earlier-prompt authentication and
new-prompt continuation, item and body fit refusals, invalid or non-progressing turns, earlier frame attachments, and
tiny bodies whose pending markers exceed the route limit. All twelve new tests passed on Python 3.12.8 and 3.9.6.

With `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 PYTHONPATH=tests`, the following modules ran on both Pythons (447 tests each):
`test_problem_result_parts_continued`, `test_problem_result_parts`, `test_problem_result_contract`,
`test_problem_initial_slot_replacement`, `test_research_problem_reviews`, `test_review_assignment_request_once`,
`test_review_state_evaluation`, `test_review_source_complete`, `test_research_complete_source_continuation`,
`test_review_result_descriptor_aliases`, `test_research_guidance`, `test_codex`, `test_manifest`,
`test_review_input_projection`, `test_research_problem_slate`, `test_review_objection_identity`,
`test_research_problem_adjudication_resolution` and `test_research_problem_objections`. This includes every test
module mentioning `material_objections` or `problem_evidence_parts`.

The two existing tests that asserted the earlier delivery rules now assert the new behavior, retaining every
assertion that still holds. No other existing test was changed in this round:

- `test_owned_body_elision_shrinks_the_prefix` checks that all five computations arrive in order, only the first
  part turn carrying a computation attaches the shared body, later computation turns carry the exact earlier-turn
  entry and note, and the final review has no missing post-gate coverage.
- `test_oversized_owned_artifact_travels_alone_and_an_unfittable_unit_is_refused` checks that, with the unit cap at
  10,000 and the input limit at 150,000, the oversized `assessment_evidence[0]` travels alone with its body attached.
  The next turn is refused with `review_input_too_large` naming `assessment_evidence[1]`, whose 200,000-character
  record item cannot fit alone. Refusal leaves the store revision unchanged.

These two tests ran first and passed on both Pythons: 2 tests in 2.242 seconds on Python 3.12.8 and 2 tests in
2.525 seconds on Python 3.9.6. The subsequent module runs each passed all 447 tests, with no failures or errors:
599.609 seconds on Python 3.12.8 and 696.504 seconds on Python 3.9.6. `codex/generate.py --check` and
`git diff --check` passed. The full suite was not run.
