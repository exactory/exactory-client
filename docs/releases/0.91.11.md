# Exactory 0.91.11

With this release, a command records its finished work while other commands use the same store. The records of
that work do not depend on the revision of the whole store, and each record waits for a busy store. They belong
to a run, a managed deposit, submission or verdict, an acquisition, a cohort collection or a full-text job. The
records around a reviewer call and the release of a Colab job take the same wait. A command that repeats a
committed request gets its receipt from a read. Each read and each commit of a command waits for the store. The
acquisition commands and the remote operations start while other commands commit. A reviewer answer is examined
again before a v7 openness chain, a finding or the openness gate builds on it. The release follows 0.91.10. Its
records are `20261119223643-finishing-writes-give-up-after-eight-seconds`,
`20261120104814-replay-and-first-writes-wait-for-readers-on-a-large-store` and
`20261120092352-next-v7-turn-reads-an-unchecked-refused-answer`, under `misc/harness-improvement/` of the
exactory repository.

## What changed

Records of finished work and other commands:

- A command writes records after its work. `exactory-lab run` writes them after its program, and a managed
  deposit, submission or verdict after each request. An acquisition writes them after its requests, and a cohort
  collection after each page.
- Before this release, each of these records was a compare-and-swap on the revision of the whole store. A write
  had at most 8 attempts within 8 seconds. A commit of another command during an attempt refused that attempt. A
  command that only read the store at the time of the commit refused the write too.
- On copies of four study stores of 94 MB to 1,004 MB, one such write took 4 to 48 seconds. On the three larger
  stores, the first refusal ended the command.
- `exactory-lab run` then spent an admission before its program started, or ended refused after its program
  ended. A deposit, a submission or a verdict kept a step with an unknown outcome, although the service answered.
  An acquisition ended without the responses of its requests, and a cohort collection without a page.
- Now each of these records commits against the records that its write read, and not against the revision of the
  store. A commit that changes none of those records does not refuse the write. When a commit changes one of
  them, the write is prepared again.
- Three records commit at the revision that the store has at the time of their write lock. They are the receipt
  of a reconciliation, the result of an acquisition and a page of a cohort collection. No commit of another
  command refuses them.
- Each record waits for a busy store, or for a changed record. The write is tried at most 600 times, and no
  attempt starts later than 600 seconds after the first one. The finish of a reviewer call and the records of a
  probe have the same wait, and all of them read one definition.
- The commit of such a record can meet a command that reads the store. The commit then waits for that read to
  end, inside the same wait, and the attempt keeps its work. Before this release, the write was rolled back after
  its commit waited 1 second. Its next attempt then checked the history of the store again.
- When a record is still refused after the wait, the command is refused with `store_busy`. `details.cause` holds
  the last refusal. Each record has its own message, which says what was recorded, what is kept, and which
  command goes on.
- For a run, the message of a record after the program names `reconcile-run` for the admission and the same
  `exactory-lab run` command. Each of them records the run from the kept files and does not run the program
  again. `details.admission_id` and `details.request_id` name both.
- For the answer of a step of a deposit, a submission or a verdict, the message says that the request was sent
  once. It also says that the service answered. `details.response` holds the members of the answer that the step
  needs, and never the whole answer. For a deposit, `exactory-draft reconcile` with the intent ID in
  `details.intent_id` reads the deposition and records the step. For a submission, `exactory reconcile` reads the
  task and records the step. A verdict has no such read, and the message says to give `details.response` to the
  user.
- For an acquisition, the message says that the operation stays admitted and that the same command with a new
  request ID replaces it. For a cohort collection, `resume` with a new request ID requests the page again or ends
  the operation.
- A step of a deposit starts after a check of the manuscript and the reviews. The request of a submission starts
  after a check of the published record. The start of such a step keeps its compare-and-swap on the revision of
  that check. When the store moved, the refusal is `stale_revision`, and when the store is busy, `store_busy`.
  Both messages now say that no request of the step was sent and that the same command checks again.
- `exactory-lab run` reconciles its own run without a revision check. Before this release, a commit of another
  command between two reads of the launcher refused the command with `stale_revision`. That refusal came after
  the program ended. `reconcile-run` keeps the check of its `--expected-revision`.
- The same wait applies to three more kinds of record. They are the last record of a direct deposit, the concept
  DOI of a version draft, and the records of a full-text job. Before this release, the two records of
  `exactory-draft` had one attempt, and a record of a full-text job was tried again for 8 seconds.
- The CLI reference describes the wait and the cases of each command. The skills `experiment`, `deposit`,
  `submit`, `verify`, `literature-review` and `cohort` say what to do after the refusal.

A claimed run whose program never started:

- `exactory-lab run` claims its admission first. It then starts a worker, records that the program is released,
  and gives the worker its token. The worker starts the program only after it reads that token.
- Before this release, a launch that ended between its claim and the release record left its admission without a
  run. The same request was then refused with `execution_recovery_required`. The way on was a record with
  `resolution: "interrupted"`, which keeps the reserved units, and a new admission.
- Now the same `exactory-lab run` command with the same request ID starts the program of such a claim. The claim
  must be a local run that no record released and that has no outcome. Its folder must hold no stream and no
  terminal outcome, and no worker of the run must be alive.
- This applies in three cases:
  - The store refused the release record after the wait.
  - The worker process did not start, or it ended before the release record.
  - The launcher ended before the release record.
- The program of an admission still runs at most once. A request with another ID is refused, as before. A claim
  whose program was released is reconciled and is not started again.
- `reconcile-run` with `resolution: "interrupted"` still records such a claim as interrupted. `reconcile-run`
  without a resolution is refused for it with `execution_recovery_required`, and the message names the
  `exactory-lab run` command that starts the program.

Records around a reviewer call and a Colab job:

- Three more kinds of record had one attempt, or 8 seconds. They are the release of a Colab job, the records
  before a reviewer program starts, and the refusal of a reviewer output. Another command that wrote to the
  store, or only read it, refused them.
- The release of a Colab job. Before this release, the record that releases the job was a compare-and-swap on the
  revision of the whole store, with one attempt. The launcher then compared two reads of the revision before it
  wrote the signal `GO`.
- A commit of another command at one of these two places ended `exactory-lab run --backend colab` before the
  runner started the program. On a fixture study with a store of 96 MB, a launch with no other command ended
  after 59 seconds with its run. Four of four launches beside a writer ended after 18 to 21 seconds without it.
- The same command then released the job. It was refused with `execution_recovery_required` until the runner
  finished, and a third command recorded the run.
- Now the release commits against the records that the check of the admission read, and it takes the wait of the
  first block. The signal `GO` follows the commit at once. A saved release without its signal gets its fresh
  check on a read during which the store takes no commit of another command.
- With this change, the launch beside a writer that commits every 3 seconds ended with its run after 48 seconds,
  in two of two trials. Beside a writer that commits every 0.5 seconds, it ended with its run after 49 seconds.
- The records before a reviewer program starts. A call on a local route starts several transport processes. They
  are the version checks, the page renders of a visual route, and the process that sends the request. The harness
  records each of them.
- A staged review task adds four records. They are the launch claim, the record that binds the worker to its
  reservation, the authorization of the worker, and the start of the worker.
- Before this release, the claim and the authorization had one attempt, and the other records were tried for 8
  seconds. A store that another command locked ended the worker or the transport process before the reviewer
  program started. The task or the turn then lost an attempt without a reviewer call.
- On copies of a study store of 94 MB, 15 of 16 launches of a staged worker completed with no other command. In
  the other launch, the polling reads of the launching command refused a record of its own worker. Beside a
  command that read the store in a loop, 0 of 6 launches completed. Beside a writer that committed every 2
  seconds, 0 of 6 completed. 10 of these 12 launches spent an attempt of the task.
- Each transport process wrote its own record, inside its time limit. On a copy of a study store of 1,004 MB,
  that record took 27.5 to 28.7 seconds. The version check of a Codex route has a limit of 15 seconds. The check
  ended at that limit, so no Codex reviewer call started on a store of that size, with no other command.
- On the same store, the version check of a Claude route ended at its limit of 30 seconds with
  `review_runtime_missing`. The version check of the page renderer of a visual route has a limit of 10 seconds.
  On a store of 604 MB, it did not end within that limit. In both cases, the call did not reach the reviewer.
- Now the command that owns the call records each transport process before that process starts its program. The
  process waits for one byte on a pipe, which the command writes after the record. As a result, the time limit of
  the program starts after the record, at every size of the store.
- A refused record does not become a failed observation of the transport. The command that owns the call is
  refused, and the transport process starts no program.
- With this change, the Codex version check on the store of 1,004 MB returned its version after 53.2 seconds,
  with one record. The Claude version check read its version after a record of 42.5 seconds. The renderer check
  on the store of 604 MB read its version after a record of 17.0 seconds.
- Each of the records before a reviewer program takes the wait of the first block. These records read no record
  before their commit, so no commit of another command refuses them. A refusal of the check inside the record
  ends it at once, for example a changed dependency of the task.
- The launch handshake of a staged worker had a limit of 60 seconds. Now the launching command waits for the
  record of the worker for as long as the worker runs, and for at most 600 seconds.
- With this change, 4 of 4 launches of a staged worker completed beside a reading command. Beside a writer that
  committed every 2 seconds, 4 of 4 completed too. Each launch wrote one record of a transport process, and its
  program ran once.
- The refusal of a reviewer output. When the answer of a reviewer fails a check of its contract, the harness
  writes a refusal record. The plan of the chain, the replacement of the answer and the notice of the next
  request read that record.
- Before this release, the record was a compare-and-swap on the revision of the whole store, with one attempt,
  and no message reported a failed attempt. The run returned without `output_refusal`. The plan then gave the
  next turn on the refused answer. A second run was refused with `review_resampling_forbidden`, and the
  replacement with `review_refusal_replacement_ineligible`.
- On the fixture of a reading chain with a store of 96 MB, a second process committed one record every 3 seconds.
  The refusal record was lost in two of two runs.
- Now the refusal record commits against the records that the check of the output read, and it takes the wait of
  the first block. The read of the store between a run and its refusal record waits for a busy store too, for at
  most 600 seconds. With this change, the same trial recorded the refusal, and the receipt of the run named it.
- Three records after a reviewer call had a wait of 600 seconds before this release. They are the capture of a
  call, the capture of a staged worker and the record of the result of a call. Now their commit also waits for a
  command that reads the store, as the first block describes. Before this release, such a commit was rolled back
  after 1 second.
- When one of these records is still refused after the wait, the refusal is `store_busy`, and `details.cause`
  holds the last refusal. Each record has its own message:
  - The release of a Colab job. The runner started no program, and the admission keeps its claim. The same
    command, or `reconcile-run` for the admission, records the release and signals the runner.
  - The record of a transport process. That process started no program, and no request was sent to the reviewer.
    `review-series`, or `review-run` with `action: "reconcile"`, records the call as interrupted.
  - The capture of a call. No record holds what the reviewer returned. The same two commands record the call as
    interrupted, and the next run calls the reviewer again.
  - The record of the result of a call. The capture stays. The same command with the same payload and request ID
    records the result without a reviewer call.
  - The refusal of an output after a run. The run and its output are recorded, and `details.refusal` holds what
    the output fails. `review-run` with the request ID in `details.request_id` returns the recorded run and
    records the refusal.
  - The refusal of an output after a refused `value-review` or `review-adjudication`. `details.recording_refusal`
    holds the refusal of that command. The same command at the current revision records the refusal of the
    output.
  - The launch claim and the authorization of a staged worker. No request was sent to the reviewer. After a
    refused claim, the same command launches again. After a refused authorization, the reconciliation records a
    known failure.
- The worker of a staged task writes the message of its own record to its log. The log is `worker.log` in the
  folder of the worker under `research/review-workers/`. `review-run` is then refused with
  `review_worker_reconciliation_required`, as for every worker that ends without a capture. The record of a
  transport process is an exception: the worker captures that refusal as a failed observation.
- `review-series` plans again after `store_busy`, as before. One case is new. When `details.request_id` of the
  refusal is the request ID of the refused action, the command runs that action again with the same request ID. A
  run whose output refusal the store did not take is such an action.
- The CLI reference describes these records in "Inputs and receipts", "Actual execution", "Review chains and
  `review-series`", "Staged problem reviews" and "Problem evidence in parts". The skills `experiment`,
  `ai-science`, `math-solver`, `verify` and `math-conjecturer` say what to do after the refusal.

A repeated request, the reads of a command and the first writes:

- A repeated request. A command with the request ID and the payload of a committed request returns the receipt of
  that request. Before this release, the store answered it inside a write transaction that changed nothing. The
  start of that transaction waited for every writer, and its commit waited for every reader.
- On a store of the size of a study, the repeat was therefore refused with `store_busy` beside another command.
  One trial used a copy of a study store of 94 MB. One process read the store in a loop, and a second process
  wrote to it. Beside them, 0 of 3 repeats returned. Each was refused after 1.1 seconds.
- Now the command answers a committed request from a read, before it takes a write lock. A write transaction that
  finds its request committed ends without a commit. With this change, 3 of 3 repeats returned on the 94 MB copy,
  in 10.6 to 14.9 seconds. On a copy of a store of 1 GB, 3 of 3 returned, in 131 to 141 seconds. No repeat sent a
  request.
- `init`, `adopt`, `problem-init`, `recover` and `exactory-cohort collect` open a store that exists without a
  write lock. Before this release, another command that read or wrote the store refused that open with
  `store_busy`.
- The reads of a command. In the first and the third block, the commit of a record waits for the reads that hold
  the store. While a commit waits, the store admits no new read. With those blocks alone, a read of another
  command that started in that time was refused with `store_busy` after 1 second.
- In the trials of the design, this refused 4 of 24 runs of `status`, `next` and `obligations` on the 94 MB copy.
  It also refused 9 of 60 first reads of a command on the 1 GB copy.
- Now a read of a command waits for a commit of another command that is in progress, for at most 600 seconds. The
  commit of every write of a command waits as long for the reads that hold the store. This applies to every
  write, and not only to the records of the first and the third block.
- With this change, the three commands ran beside the same reader and writer. On the 94 MB copy, 24 of 24 runs
  completed, in 6.0 to 11.9 seconds. On the 1 GB copy, 9 of 9 completed, in 46 to 71 seconds. Of 60 first reads
  on the 1 GB copy, 60 were admitted. Ten of them waited for more than 1 second, and the longest wait was 8.03
  seconds.
- The wait applies to the commands of `exactory-research`, `exactory`, `exactory-draft`, `exactory-lab` and
  `exactory-cohort`, and to the worker of a staged review task.
- The first writes. The first write of an acquisition command admits its operation. Before this release, it was a
  compare-and-swap on `--expected-revision`, so each commit of another command refused it with `stale_revision`.
  A busy store refused it after 1 second.
- Now the admission commits when the store is at `--expected-revision` or at a later revision, and it takes the
  wait of the first block. This applies to `collect`, `resume`, `acquire`, `expand`, `fulltext`,
  `import-fulltext`, `fulltext-resume` and `import-response`. `fulltext-start` starts its job under the same
  condition, with the same wait.
- When `--expected-revision` is later than the revision of the store, the command is refused with
  `stale_revision`. Its message says to read the revision with `status`. When the store still refuses the
  admission after the wait, the command is refused with `store_busy`. The message says that the command sent no
  request and that the same request ID goes on.
- A managed deposit, submission or verdict first saves the intent of its remote operation. Before this release,
  that save was a compare-and-swap on a revision that the command read itself, or on `--expected-revision`. Now
  `exactory submit`, `exactory verify` and `exactory-draft deposit` without `--expected-revision` save the intent
  while other commands commit. The save takes the wait of the first block. With `--expected-revision`, each of
  the three commands saves the intent only at that revision.
- In one trial, a cohort collection, an acquisition and the save of a remote intent started together on the 1 GB
  copy. With the first three blocks alone, their first writes were refused 7, 41 and 36 times. The acquisition
  was not admitted, and the trial took 3,406 seconds. With this block, no first write was refused, and the trial
  took 593 seconds.
- The CLI reference describes these rules in "Inputs and receipts" and in "Review, publication, and
  verification". The skills `literature-review` and `cohort` say which request ID goes on after a refused
  admission.

An answer that failed its check:

- `review-run` examines the answer of a reviewer against the contract of its turn. For an answer that fails, it
  writes a refusal record. That record is a second write after the run. The third block makes this write wait for
  a busy store. The record can still be missing, for example when the command ends from outside before the write.
- A chain under the reading protocol v7 relied on that record alone. Without the record, the next turn, the
  verification of the chain and `problem-openness` read the answer as an examined one. The protocols v4 to v6
  examine an answer again before they build on it.
- In the runs of the design, 16 answers that fail their contract had no refusal record. Some of them ended
  `review-assignment`, `problem-openness` and `review-series` with a Python error. Others were used. The chain
  went on over the answer, and a later chain carried the turn. `problem-openness` recorded the finding of a final
  turn that fails its contract.
- One such finding states the outcome `supported_open` and names a ground. The contract refuses that pair. Once
  it was recorded, the openness gate reported no obligation.
- A problem openness review of one turn had the same defect. The openness gate did not read the answer of such a
  review again.
- The defect is in 0.91.0 to 0.91.10 for a v7 chain, and in 0.90.0 to 0.91.10 for a review of one turn.
- Now each of these steps examines the answer again before it reads it. `review-assignment` of the next turn is
  refused with `review_source_continuation_unverified`. The verification of the chain is refused with
  `openness_reading_incomplete`, and `problem-openness` with `review_context_unverified`. Each refusal names the
  turn in `details.assignment_id` and the failure in `details.refusal`.
- A new chain carries no turn of such a chain from that turn on.
- `review-series` plans the state `resume` for such an answer. The step is the recorded `review-run` request with
  its request ID. That command calls no reviewer, returns the recorded run and records the refusal. For a v7
  turn, the plan after it is the replacement that carries the refusal.
- `problem-openness` examines the final turn of a v6 chain and a review of one turn in the same way. Their plan
  is the same `resume`. Before this release, a malformed v6 final ended the command and the plan with a Python
  error.
- For a finding from a review of one turn, the openness gate now reads the answer again. The recorded answer must
  be the selected answer of the review, and it must pass its check. If not, the finding is
  `problem_openness_stale` with the cause `openness_native_review_unverified`. When the store refuses a read of
  that check, the cause is the code of that refusal.
- A part of an argument at a final turn is no finding. `problem-openness` refuses it with
  `openness_reading_incomplete`, as it refuses a reading response. Before this release, the command ended with a
  Python error.
- The CLI reference describes this in the new item "An answer that failed its check and has no refusal record" of
  "The reading protocol v7". The row of the state `resume` and the entries of the three codes name the new case.

## Effects and limits

Records of finished work:

- The wait applies to each record, and not to the command. After its claim, `exactory-lab run` writes one record
  before the program and three after it. A managed verdict writes three such records, and a cohort collection one
  for each page. As a result, one command can wait for longer than 600 seconds.
- An attempt that starts inside the wait runs to its end. On a large store one attempt takes tens of seconds, so
  the refusal can come later than 600 seconds after the first attempt.
- During a wait, the command keeps what it owns. A second launch of the admission is refused with
  `execution_live`, and a second deposit command with `remote_operation_owned`. A second acquisition of the same
  target is refused with `acquisition_in_progress`. An admitted acquisition keeps its request allowance.
- During an attempt, the write holds the write lock of the store and checks the history of the store once. A
  write of another command that starts in that time is refused with `store_busy` after 1 second. A write with the
  wait of this block is then tried again.
- The commit of such a record can wait for a reader. A write of another command that starts during that wait is
  refused with `store_busy` after 1 second. A read of another command waits for the commit to end.
- The first write of a command keeps its compare-and-swap and its one attempt, except the first writes that the
  fourth block of "What changed" names. A command that is refused there with `stale_revision` or `store_busy`
  wrote nothing. Run it again.
- The record that releases a program reads the records of the admission check. A command that changes such
  records for longer than the wait still refuses it. The same `exactory-lab run` command then starts the program.
- A verdict whose answer the store refused for the whole wait has no receipt, and no read of the server confirms
  it. With a pending step, `exactory verify` does not take the managed path and sends the verdict directly. The
  message and the `verify` skill say not to run the command again for that verdict.
- When a record of a full-text job is still refused after the wait, the invocation ends. `fulltext-resume` then
  returns the job as pending, with the code `store_busy` in `pending`. If the store also refuses the record that
  ends the invocation, the command is refused.

Records around a reviewer call and a Colab job:

- The wait applies to each record. A call on a Codex route writes four records of transport processes before its
  request: three version checks and the process that sends the request. A call on a Claude route writes two. The
  capture and the record of the result follow.
- On a large store, these records take time before the request. On a copy of a study store of 1,004 MB, under
  load, the four records of a Codex call took 40.3 to 46.9 seconds each. The process that sends the request
  started 178 seconds after the start of the step. That time is now outside the time limit of the reviewer.
- A staged task writes four records before its worker calls the reviewer, and then the records of the transport
  processes. During a wait, the command keeps its locks and the capacity of the call, so another call of the
  store waits for that capacity.
- The controller of a staged task waits for the capture for the timeout of the route plus 15 seconds, from the
  end of the launch. The start record of the worker, the records of its transport processes and its capture are
  inside that time. On a large store, `review-run` can end once with "Resume the owned reservation; its outcome
  is still pending". The capture is durable, and the same `review-run` finds it.
- A refusal after the wait has a cost. A staged task uses one of its attempts. An ordinary call is recorded as
  interrupted, and that outcome has a limit of eight attempts. The admission of a Colab run stays claimed.
- A commit of one of these records can wait for a reader. A write of another command that starts during that wait
  is refused with `store_busy` after 1 second. A read of another command waits for the commit to end.
- The refusal of an output can stay without its record in three cases. The read of the store after the run can
  stay refused for its whole wait. A command can end from outside during a wait. The provenance of the run can be
  unavailable. In the first two cases, the same `review-run` request records the refusal.
- After the first of these cases, `review-run` is refused with the `store_busy` of the store, which names no
  request in its details. `review-series` then makes a new plan. For the answers of the fifth block of "What
  changed", that plan is the state `resume`.
- After the runner of a Colab job finishes, the launcher reads the store once before it records the run. A busy
  store at that read refuses `exactory-lab run` with the `store_busy` of the store. The same command then records
  the run.
- The fresh check of a saved release without its signal holds the store for the time of that check. On the copy
  of the store of 94 MB, the read with its copy took 2.5 to 3.4 seconds. A write of another command that starts
  in that time waits, or is refused with `store_busy` after 1 second.

A repeated request, the reads of a command and the first writes:

- A command can now wait for the store for 600 seconds at each read and at each commit, where it was refused
  after 1 second. Such a wait is usually as long as one read of the store by another command. That time grows
  with the history of the study. In the trials, the first reads on the 1 GB copy waited for 8.03 seconds at most.
- A tool that ends a command before the end of such a wait gets no message. The store did not record the write
  that waited. The same request can run again.
- After 600 seconds, the read or the commit is refused with `store_busy` and the message of the store. The
  records that the command wrote before stay.
- Three kinds of access keep one attempt of 1 second. The first is a read that a command repeats while it waits
  for a worker or for a runner. The second is the render of the output views. The third is an access outside the
  five programs, for example by the math-solver controller `exactory-math`.
- The wait for the write lock at the start of a write stays 1 second. A write of another command that starts
  while a commit waits for a reader is therefore refused with `store_busy`. A write with the wait of the first
  block is then tried again.
- The math-solver controller keeps two reads that hold the research store for some time. The commits of other
  commands now wait for these reads. The reads of the controller itself keep one attempt. How long these reads
  hold the store was not measured.
- A repeated request still reads the history of the store two times, and three times for an acquisition. On the 1
  GB copy, a repeat took 131 to 141 seconds beside the reader and the writer.
- Each transaction still checks the whole history of the store. A lock of a study store is therefore held for
  tens of seconds. This release makes the commands wait for each other, and it does not shorten a transaction.
- The acquisition commands no longer compare the revision that you read with the store. A command with a second
  request ID for a target is admitted also when another command finished that target after your last read. It
  then sends its requests again. Before you acquire a target, make sure that no other command acquired it after
  your last read.
- `visual` and `import-oai-cohort` keep the compare-and-swap on `--expected-revision`. So do the claim of a run,
  the start of a step of a deposit or of a submission, and the JSON mutations of `exactory-research`. Beside a
  command that commits steadily, each commit of that command still refuses such a write with `stale_revision`.
- `import-response` is admitted at a later revision, and it records its result with a compare-and-swap on the
  revision that it read after its admission. When another command commits in between, the command is refused with
  `stale_revision`. It sent no request, and the operation stays admitted. Run the command again with a new
  request ID.

An answer that failed its check:

- The check runs where a v7 chain, `problem-openness` and the openness gate read an answer. Two views do not
  apply it. `series.json` shows an answer that fails its check as the selected run of its turn while the store
  holds no refusal record for it. The step `authentication` of `record-check` lists that turn as ready.
- A chain that 0.91.0 to 0.91.10 continued over such an answer gets refusals and no plan of its own.
  `review-assignment` of its next turn is refused with a message that names the earlier turn and the new export.
  A replacement of one of its later turns is still recorded. That replacement takes one reviewer call, and the
  call gives no finding.
- `review-series` plans `resume` only when no other state of the chain comes first. A pending call, an unverified
  context and the handoff after a denser ratio come first. Follow the plan that the command gives.
- The check judges a stored answer by the contract of the installed release. The contract of a v7 turn accepts
  the same answers in 0.91.0 to 0.91.10, so an answer that one of these releases accepted passes now.
- The reading turns of the protocols v4 to v6 and of a math chain keep their behavior. `review-assignment` of the
  next turn is refused with the code of the contract, and the plan is a stop with that code. Series in parts and
  evidence parts get no `resume` step.
- The check does not run at the place where the harness selects the answer of an assignment for every protocol.
  The design assumes that a check at that place judges the stored answers of the protocols v1 to v5 by the
  contracts of today. It also assumes that the commands then report other results for stored records. No run
  measured this.
- Each check reads the packet and the answer of one turn. The design measured a fixture chain of four reading
  turns and a final over three texts of 150,000 characters. One check took 0.01 to 0.07 seconds on a loaded
  machine. The verification of that chain took 1.46 seconds, of which the checks took 0.26 seconds.
- With this release, two precautions of 0.90.0 to 0.91.10 are no longer necessary. Other commands can use a store
  while `review-series` drives an openness review there. A finding also needs no repeated `review-run` requests
  before the post of its problem.

## Compatibility

No file of the runtime identity of a reviewer route changes, and no reviewer prompt or packet changes. As a
result, no route becomes stale.

Records of finished work:

- Stored records keep their members and their meaning. The records of a run that this release writes have the
  members that 0.91.6 gives them.
- The outcome of a run names the revision of its commit in `recorded_revision`, as before. The record that
  releases a program names the revision before its commit, as before. Both values are set at the commit.
- Request IDs and operation names do not change. A request that an earlier release committed returns its receipt.
- One stored payload differs. The payload of a new `acquisition.finish` event holds the result without
  `revision`. The result, the operation record and the printed result hold `revision`, as before.
- A run, an intent or an operation that an earlier release left unfinished is finished by the commands that
  finished it before. One case differs: a claim that an earlier release left without a release record is started
  by the same request. To end such a claim without a run, record it as interrupted.
- Some refusals have a new form. Before this release, these commands were refused with `stale_revision` or
  `store_busy` after 8 seconds, with the message of the store. Now the refusal is `store_busy` after the wait,
  with the message of the record and `details.cause`. For a run or a remote step, the details also hold the
  members that the message names.
- The refusal `execution_recovery_required` of a worker that did not start has a new message. Its details hold
  `admission_id` and `request_id` beside `errno`.

Records around a reviewer call and a Colab job:

- Stored records keep their members and their meaning. Four kinds of record have the members that 0.91.7 gives
  them. They are the release of a Colab job, the identity of a transport process in `children`, a reservation,
  and the refusal of an output.
- `recorded_revision` of a refusal record is the revision of its commit, as before. The value is now set at the
  commit, so it stays right when other commands commit before it.
- Request IDs, operation names and stored payloads do not change. A request that an earlier release committed
  returns its receipt.
- Unfinished work of an earlier release is finished by the commands that finished it before. A Colab claim
  without a release is released by the same command or by `reconcile-run`. A reservation with a launch claim and
  a call without an outcome are reconciled as before.
- A run of an earlier release can have an output that fails its check and no refusal record. `review-run` with
  the request ID of that run returns the run and records the refusal.
- Some refusals have a new form. The refusal `stale_revision` with the message "Research changed while checking a
  missing remote execution signal" does not occur any more. A run that waited for the record of its result ended
  with the last refusal of the store. Now it ends with `store_busy`, and `details.cause` holds that refusal.
- A recording command whose output refusal stays unrecorded after the wait is refused with `store_busy`. Before
  this release, it was refused with its own refusal, which `details.recording_refusal` now holds.
- The process that the harness puts in front of a transport process has a new command line. A command starts that
  process from the tree of its own release. A release that is installed beside the earlier one leaves a running
  call on its own tree.

A repeated request, the reads of a command and the first writes:

- No stored byte changes. No record gains or loses a member, and no operation name, request ID or payload of a
  stored event changes. A request that an earlier release committed returns the same receipt, now from a read.
- `admission_revision` of an acquisition operation and `revision` of its result name the revision of the commit,
  as before. Before this release, that revision was always `--expected-revision` plus one. Now it is later when
  another command committed first.
- `created_revision` of a saved intent is the revision before its commit, as before.
- `exactory submit` and `exactory verify` without `--expected-revision` no longer take the current revision of
  the store for the intent. With `--expected-revision`, they compare it with the store, as before.
- `exactory-draft deposit` without `--expected-revision` no longer takes the revision of its readiness report for
  the intent. Each step of the deposit still starts at the revision of its own check.
- A store can hold the journal of a write whose process ended in the middle of its commit. A command that opened
  the store before that, and then writes, is now refused with `store_recovery_required`. Before this release, the
  write rolled that journal back by itself. `recover` and the open of a store for creation still roll it back.
- Some refusals have a new form. A first write of an acquisition that waited is refused with `store_busy` and a
  message of its own, with `details.cause`. The refusal `stale_revision` of an acquisition has the details
  `expected_revision` and `revision`, and a new message, when the store is behind `--expected-revision`.
- `collect` for a collection that another command created after your last read is now refused with
  `collection_exists`. Before this release, the refusal was `stale_revision`.

An answer that failed its check:

- No stored record changes its form, and no kind of record or member is new. A refusal record that the `resume`
  step writes is the record that the run writes.
- No code is new. Three codes have one more case: `review_source_continuation_unverified`,
  `openness_reading_incomplete` and `review_context_unverified`. The plan state `resume` has `details` in the new
  case.
- A chain whose answers pass their checks takes the same assignments, packets, prompts and plans as before, and
  it verifies as before.
- A chain that 0.91.0 to 0.91.10 recorded can hold an answer that fails its check and has no refusal record. At
  the head of the chain, the plan is `resume`. When the chain went on over the answer, it takes no further turn
  and gives no finding. When a later chain carried the turn, that chain does not verify.
- A finding that 0.91.0 to 0.91.10 recorded from such a chain shows as `problem_openness_stale` with the cause
  `openness_reading_incomplete` in `status`. A finding that 0.90.0 to 0.91.10 recorded from such a review of one
  turn shows as `problem_openness_stale` with the cause `openness_native_review_unverified`.
- For such a finding, export a new packet with the same `reviewer_id` and assign it. A new v7 chain can carry the
  reading turns before the answer. A finding without that obligation needs no step.
- The design examined 35 stores of the project on 2026-11-20. None held an assignment of a v7 chain or of a
  review of one turn under the template `exactory-review-v7/native_math`.
