# Exactory 0.47.0

This release lets the user change the fixed objective of a research study. The
change needs the user's instruction as a pinned file in the study's `context/`
folder. The harness checks the pinned file and its bytes. It does not check who
wrote them, so an agent records only the user's own words.

## A user-authorized change of the research objective

A research study fixes its complete objective with `exactory-research target`.
Before this release, every later change of that objective failed with
`objective_locked`. Only a development round could widen the objective. A study
that established a valid result for part of the objective therefore could not
reach research readiness when the rest had no clear route, even when the user
decided to narrow the objective.

`target` now accepts an optional `authorization` field. Its value is the `id` of
an `artifact` pin whose path is under `context/` and whose bytes hold the user's
explicit instruction. With it, a fixed research objective can change:

- The new objective needs a new `id`. Its statement must differ from the earlier
  one after casefolding and whitespace collapsing. Otherwise the change fails
  with `objective_locked`.
- One instruction authorizes one change. A second change with the same bytes
  fails with `objective_locked`.
- An unknown pin, a pin outside `context/`, an empty file, or `authorization` on
  a first or unchanged target fails with `invalid_target`.
- The earlier objective stays in `research_objective`.
  `objective_lineage/{new id}` records `{id, predecessor, relation:
  "user_authorized", reason, authorization: {id, path, artifact, text}, round_id:
  null}`. Earlier checkpoints therefore stay inheritable.
- The harness does not check the direction of the change. The reason states it.
- The readiness candidate and the round review packet carry every
  user-authorized change in the current lineage as `objective_changes`, with the
  user's words. Reviewers therefore see that the objective changed and why.
  Studies without such a change keep their candidate digests.
- The change alters the configuration that the synthesis and every assessment
  bind. `standards`, `rationale`, `context` and `innovation` report
  `synthesis_dependencies_stale` until they are recorded again. A successor that
  inherits an assessment made under the earlier objective fails with
  `inherited_result_stale`. An earlier result is inherited only through a new
  assessment with `inheritance_refresh`, as after a widening.

Without `authorization`, a changed objective fails with `objective_locked`, as
before. Verification repinning is unchanged. The research constitution is
unchanged, so existing studies do not need to adopt a new policy.

A math-solver search bound to the study workspace refuses a changed objective
with `research_objective_mismatch`, as it does after a widening.

## Other changes

- The widening of an objective through a round now also compares statements
  after casefolding and whitespace collapsing, so a whitespace-only change is not
  a wider objective.
- `normalized_text` in `research_harness/operations.py` is the one statement
  normalizer; `development.py` uses it.

## How to use it

1. Save the user's own words as a file in the study's `context/` folder.
2. Pin it with `exactory-research artifact`.
3. Run `exactory-research target` with the new objective, the reason and the
   pin's `id` as `authorization`.
4. Record the four synthesis sections again. Assess the earlier cycles again
   under the new objective, in lineage order with `inheritance_refresh`, before
   a successor inherits them.
