# Exactory 0.85.3

This release makes the time budget of `record-check` bound the processor time of each reader instead of its elapsed
time. It follows 0.85.2. The failure, the decision and the measurements are recorded in
`misc/harness-improvement/20261110222034-record-check-budget-fails-working-reader-under-load` of the exactory
repository.

## Why

The comparison of 0.85.0 and 0.85.1 ran `record-check` on two clones of `studies/2402.14112` (11 GB, 4,758 artifacts
and 11,478 workspace descriptors). The machine also ran other comparisons, test suites and research jobs, at load
averages of 6 to 8. Under 0.85.0, the `status` reader of the `report` step stopped at the default budget of 3,600
seconds with `record_command_timeout`. In the same run, `next` took 1,990.9 seconds and `obligations` took 1,265.6
seconds. Under 0.85.1, the three readers took 1,239.0, 1,237.7 and 1,241.2 seconds. The three readers compute the same
status report of the same clone, so the difference came from the machine and not from the record or the release.

The budget exists to catch a runaway cost, but it measured elapsed time. Elapsed time also contains the time that a
reader waits while other processes use the processor, the disk or the memory. A working reader could then fail the
check on a loaded machine, and the record stayed on its older release. The ledger entry
`integration-record-check-default-budget` of 0.73.2 recorded the same class at the earlier default of 900 seconds.

A measurement on 2026-11-10 shows how much processor time the reader needs. The `status` reader of
`studies/2402.14112`, the largest live record, used 1,202.6 and 1,231.2 seconds of processor time in 1,258.1 and
1,280.2 seconds, at load averages of 6 to 7. The reader is bound by the processor. A fixed pure-Python workload used
3.1 to 3.7 times more processor time on the efficiency cores (`taskpolicy -b`) than at the normal policy. On the
efficiency cores alone, the `status` reader would therefore use about 4,600 seconds, more than the old default.

## What changed

- `--time-budget` bounds the processor time (user and system time) of each reader. The processor time of a reader
  is the processor time of the check process during the reader, plus that of the child processes that ended during
  the reader. Elapsed time alone never fails a reader.
- `status`, `next` and `obligations` run in the check process under the processor-time timer `ITIMER_PROF`
  (signal `SIGPROF`) in place of the real-time timer. After a reader returns, the check compares its processor time
  with the budget.
- A `SIGPROF` that arrives after a report reader returns and before the check disarms the timer counts as the
  reader's timeout. Before this release, such a signal could leave the check as an uncaught exception. The same was
  possible with the real-time timer since 0.73.0. The check restores the previous timer and handler in every path.
- Each math-solver reader runs as a child process with no elapsed timeout. A short launcher arms `ITIMER_PROF` with
  the budget and then replaces itself with the reader command (`os.execv`). The timer stays armed across `execv`, and
  `SIGPROF` ends the reader at the budget. A reader that ends on `SIGPROF`, or whose processor time is larger than
  the budget, fails with `record_command_timeout`. Any other nonzero exit stays `record_math_read_failed`. The
  `command` of the result is the reader command without the launcher. The launcher replaces `preexec_fn`, which is
  not safe in a process with threads.
- The launcher uses the timer and not the processor-time limit `RLIMIT_CPU`. On macOS 27 with Python 3.12 and 3.9, a
  child that made a system call on every pass of its loop used 6.03 seconds of processor time under an `RLIMIT_CPU`
  limit of 1 second and received no signal. Under a timer of 1 second, the same child ended on `SIGPROF` after 1.11 to
  1.19 seconds.
- The default budget is 10,800 seconds of processor time, about 2.4 times the estimate for the `status` reader on
  the efficiency cores alone. The constant `DEFAULT_TIME_BUDGET_SECONDS` holds it, and the CLI parser reads it.
- The budget is at most 100,000,000 seconds, the largest interval timer that macOS accepts. On macOS 27, 1e8 is
  accepted, 100,000,001 is refused with `EINVAL`, and 1e19 raises `OverflowError`. A larger budget now ends with
  `invalid_input` before any copy of the record, in place of a traceback.
- The message of `record_command_timeout` names the reader and the budget. It says that the reader used more
  processor time than the budget and that `record-check` decided nothing about the record, and it tells the agent to
  run `record-check` again with a larger `--time-budget`. The details give `command`, `budget_seconds`,
  `processor_seconds` and `seconds`, and a math-solver reader adds `attack_root`. For a math-solver reader, `command`
  is now the reader command, as in `record_math_read_failed`, in place of the bare name `status` or `next`.
- Every step row and every reader row gives `processor_seconds` next to `seconds`.
- `docs/research-cli.md` describes the measure of the budget, its default and its maximum, the two times of each row
  and the meaning of the timeout, in the section on `record-check`.

## What stays the same

- The flag `--time-budget` and the code `record_command_timeout`. The code stays the same for every reader, because
  the recovery is the same.
- The 60-second limit of a page render keeps its elapsed measure, as 0.85.2 decided. It bounds an external tool, its
  worst measured time is 8.61 seconds at a load average of 64, and its module is pinned by the Codex visual transport.
- No file that a reviewer route pins changes, so no route becomes stale.
- No record, review, gate or obligation changes. A run in which every reader stays within the budget gives the same
  verdict as 0.85.2.

## What stored records see

- Nothing changes in a stored record. No migration is necessary. A report of `record-check` has the new field
  `processor_seconds` in each row.

## Known limits

- The timer launcher sets the default action of `SIGPROF` before it arms the timer. A reader that installs its own
  `SIGPROF` handler, or ignores the signal, is not stopped at the budget. It still fails when it ends, because the
  check compares its processor time with the budget.
- The behavior of the timer across `execv` on macOS rests on the probes of the record, not on a vendor page.

## Checks

Before the pull request, on Python 3.12 and 3.9:

- The new tests of `tests/test_research_record_check.py` (`ProcessorBudgetTests`) use real timers and real child
  processes. Each busy reader has a cap of 8 seconds of processor time, so a missing timer fails the test instead of
  hanging it. On 0.85.2 they fail. With this release:
  - A report or math-solver reader that sleeps past its budget passes.
  - A report or math-solver reader that keeps the processor busy fails with `record_command_timeout` before its cap,
    for both loop shapes (a clock read on every pass, and a clock read once per block of work).
  - A math-solver reader sees an armed `ITIMER_PROF` of at most the budget.
  - A deadline that arrives while the timer is disarmed counts as the timeout, and the previous handler is restored.
  - Budgets of 100,000,001 and 1e19 end with `invalid_input` and no copy, through `check_record` and through the
    command, and 1e8 passes the validation.
- The changed tests patch the processor clock in place of `time.monotonic` to decide a timeout, and the test of the
  default asserts 10,800 seconds. The measured durations of the 0.73.2 ledger entry still pass the default.
- `tests.test_research_record_check`, the incident ledger, the CLI modules, the check-mode audit, the Codex,
  manifest, guidance and release-record modules, and every test module that imports `record_check`.
- `python -m compileall -q research_harness bin`, `codex/generate.py --check`, `git diff --check`, and
  `ci/check_release_records.py --release 0.85.3`.

CI on Python 3.9, 3.12 and 3.14 runs after the pull request opens.
