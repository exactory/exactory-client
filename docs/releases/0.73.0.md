# Exactory 0.73.0

This release closes the harness problems that research runs met again and again, and it adds the checks that keep
them closed. One command now runs a review chain to its end. A failed reviewer call has an outcome that decides the
next step. An answer whose only defect is its framing is kept. A stuck parts series continues in parts v2, and a
posted problem stays posted. A record can be checked offline with a new version before it moves to that version.
Every recorded incident is in a regression ledger. The work is recorded in
`misc/harness-improvement/20261104074403-harness-problems-closed-for-good` of the exactory repository.

## Why

Research sessions found harness defects in the middle of their work, and each repair became a release in the middle
of the research. The owner ordered that a problem that occurred once is closed for good, with no regressions. A
survey of the live records and of the earlier harness records found these causes:

- Nothing ran a release against the real records before research met it. A record could not show that a newer
  version was safe for it, so the records stayed on versions from 0.69.2 to 0.72.2.
- The harness gave no executable path through a review chain. Each research session wrote its own driver. The graph
  rigidity record holds 62 tool scripts, with fixed turn limits (16, 30 and 48) and its own retry rules.
- A failed reviewer call kept no reason that a driver could act on. Of 111 calls without a usable output, 37 were
  provider conditions or looked like them. All 37 were recorded as `review_transport_failure`, and the attempts
  recorded no time.
- 24 answers lost a whole call although they had exactly one reading: 14 closed the answer object early, 8 added
  closing braces, and 2 held a raw control character in a string.
- A parts v1 series without a final had no exit when its replay outgrew the route (the bunkbed record at revision
  1546).
- A posted problem lost its terminal state under later releases, and a problem posted without `gate post` could not
  be recorded.
- The incident catalog holds 260 incident rows from 88 records in 16 classes. Only 49 rows had a test of the real
  failing shape.

## What changed

### One command runs a review chain

- `exactory-research review-series --assignment HEAD` executes the planned actions of one review chain in process,
  through the same operations as the CLI. It continues until the chain is done or needs the agent. It has no turn
  limit, and it prints one JSON line per step.
- Exit status 0 means that the chain is done. Exit status 3 is a handoff or a stop. The last line then holds the plan
  with its `code`, its `details` and, for a handoff, the command that the agent runs. Exit status 4 means that the
  next call waits more than 30 minutes. The last line then holds the plan with `not_before`. A shorter wait sleeps and
  plans again.
- The command stops with `review_series_no_progress` when it would repeat an action while the chain makes no
  progress. It plans again after `store_busy`, `stale_revision` or `review_task_dependency_changed`, at most 8 times
  in a row.
- `plan_next` gives each chain head one of ten states: `run`, `resume`, `wait`, `continue`, `replace`, `plan`,
  `record`, `handoff`, `done` and `stop`. `status` and `gate` attach the planned action to each obligation that names
  a review assignment. `status` also lists a chain head that ended without an output.
- A handoff names the command that the agent runs. For example, an openness final that `problem-openness` does not
  accept is the handoff `openness_review_not_approved`, with the command `export`, kind `problem-openness`. Its
  details hold the reviewer's decision and reason and the refusal of `problem-openness`.
- After two refused outputs, `review_slot_exhausted` names the step that the head admits:
  `review-output-correction` for a result reassessment that it accepts, an export of kind `problem-openness` for an
  openness chain, `search review-packet` for another native mathematical review, and `review-assignment` for an
  initial bar, whose slot takes a new reviewer. A bar continuation, a slate, a result and an adjudicator keep their
  slot, so for them the plan is a stop with this code.
- The skills tell the agent to run `review-series` instead of its own driver, turn limit or retry loop.

### A failed reviewer call has an outcome

- New observations record `observed_at`. A failed Codex call with an empty answer keeps `provider_diagnostic`, the
  error of its session rollout (for example `server_overloaded` or `usage_limit_exceeded`).
- Each failed call gets an outcome, and the outcome decides the next step:
  - A usage limit waits until its stated reset.
  - A model at capacity or an API error waits 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 30 minutes. The 7th failure stops with
    `review_provider_unavailable`.
  - A malformed, incomplete or unverified answer gets one more call. The second failure of the same outcome stops
    with its code.
  - An answer that its recording command refuses also gets one more call. After the second refusal,
    `review_slot_exhausted` names the next step, or stops when the slot cannot take another reviewer.
  - An answer over the output cap is replaced only when a smaller request is proven to fit. Otherwise it stops with
    `review_output_over_cap` and the measured tokens.
  - A run that the route's time limit stopped ends with `review_timeout`.
  - 8 failures of any kind at one turn position stop with `review_turn_failures_exhausted`.
- The counts come from the stored attempts, so a new run of `review-series` continues them.
- A transient failure of the reviewer CLI's version check is retried after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 seconds, before any
  model process starts. A missing runtime waits on the provider schedule with its own count.

### An answer with one reading is kept

- A completed answer that the transport cannot decode is recovered under the protocol
  `exactory-review-answer-recovery-v1` when it has exactly one reading. The rules are `surplus_closing_braces`,
  `premature_root_close` and `raw_control_characters`.
- The attempt keeps a certificate (`answer_recovery`), and authentication repeats the recovery. A duplicate key, a
  non-finite number, nesting deeper than 512 and every other reading stay refused.
- Attempts recorded before this release stay as recorded.

### No stuck parts series

- A parts v1 result series whose planner refuses with `review_input_too_large` after the replay reduction continues
  in a fresh parts v2 series in the same slot. The successor records `review_parts_restart`. A v1 continuation that
  fits is unchanged byte for byte.
- On the stuck bunkbed copy (revision 1546), slot A starts a v2 series: read turn 1, 286 units, an estimated 27 reads
  and the final. The v1 continuation of slot B fits (1,234,939 of 1,300,000 characters) and continues in v1.
- Each result slot counts only the final of its current series. A new result of a superseded series is refused with
  `review_series_superseded`. An observed final of the current series that is not recorded is the obligation
  `review_result_pending`.
- A parts v2 series keeps its read allowance at or above `unit_allowance_floor`, so that the remaining read turns fit
  the state budget. A read at the floor that overflows the output cap stops with `review_parts_budget_exhausted`
  before another call.

### Posted problems stay posted

- When the store holds a post of its current statement, `next` is `problem_already_posted` alone. `status` lists the
  findings of the current version under `after_post`, so that nothing is hidden.
- `problem-post-external` records a post made outside the harness: the exactory.ai problem id, the statement digest,
  the reason and the evidence. The post has `gate` `bypassed`.

### A record is checked before it moves to a new version

- `exactory-research record-check --workspace W` checks a copy of a record offline with the installed version. It
  never opens the SQLite store of `W`. It copies `W` up to three times, until one copy is consistent.
- Six steps run in order on the copy: `snapshot`, `integrity`, `reconstruction`, `authentication`, `continuation`
  and `report`. The first failed step ends the check.
- The continuation step plans every review chain. It runs only `review-assignment`, `value-review`,
  `review-adjudication` and `problem-openness`, and only on the copy. A reviewer call is never made: its request is
  rebuilt and checked.
- Network connections, acquisitions, publication and worker launches are refused. The report lists each local read
  that the check permits: the reviewer CLI version (`codex --version`), the runtime fingerprint of a route, and PDF
  page rendering inside the copy.
- The math-solver controllers inside the copy are read with `exactory-math search status` and `search next`. The
  check never follows the registry of absolute paths.
- A refusal for route capacity is a decision stop, not a failure. An example is a mandatory final input of a reading
  chain that is over the route limit. The next step is then a route with a larger limit, or less material.
- `status`, `next` and `obligations` each run within `--time-budget` seconds (900 by default). The report gives each
  time.
- The command prints a JSON report. It exits with 0 when the record is ready, and with 1 otherwise. The report's
  `runtime` names the version, the harness path and the Python that ran.
- On macOS, the check runs under `sandbox-exec` when its profile applies. Otherwise the report says `isolation`
  `unavailable`, with the reason.
- Before an existing record uses a new version, run `record-check` with that version. This holds for a record whose
  wrapper pins a version and for one that runs the harness from PATH. When the report says `ready` true, update the
  record's wrapper or command, make sure that it runs the new version, and start a new session. When `ready` is
  false, the record stays on the version that it runs.
- `ci/record_fingerprint.py` prints, for a record copy and a harness tree, the state that the readers of 0.72.4
  and later versions share: the assignment states, the effective attempts, the refusal codes, the authentication of
  every attempt and the rebuilt request digests. Two runs compare two versions.

### Less repeated work in the report commands

- Within one command, the readers reuse verified artifacts and series derivations through the existing evaluation
  arena (64 MiB, with eviction). Nothing outlives the command.
- On the bunkbed copy, one `status` read 10,170 artifacts, parsed 12,598 packets and rebuilt 1,431 requests before
  this change, and 5,481, 3,306 and 349 after it. The outputs are byte-identical.

### Smaller repairs

- Polling reads tolerate a busy store until their original deadline. A busy handshake keeps its reservation for the
  same request instead of a second launch.
- The general `--check` mode permits the same two local reads. A preparation that needs the reviewer CLI version or
  PDF page rendering is checked instead of refused, and it still writes nothing.
- `ci/check_release_records.py` reports tags without a GitHub release, release notes without a tag, and notes that
  the README does not link. The release procedure runs it after each release.
- The note of 0.57.0 has a dated correction of its sentence about inline formulas.
- The lab, guidance and cohort tests no longer depend on machine load, on `TMPDIR` or on writes into the source
  tree.
- `tests/incidents/ledger.json` lists 296 incidents with their expected transitions and their tests: 104 with a test
  of the real failing shape, 129 with a test of the same code path, and 63 with no test. A fix from 0.73.0 on must
  have a test of its real shape, and the two other counts cannot grow.

## What stays as it was

- The eleven route-pinned transport modules, `FIT_ORDER` and `EARLIER_FIT_ORDERS` are unchanged byte for byte.
  Recorded requests rebuild byte for byte, and recorded attempts authenticate as before.
- No reviewer prompt and no response contract changed.
- `problem-posted` keeps its contract: it requires the approved exact export.
- A staged review still cannot post, because `review_quality_measurement_pending` has no passing qualification yet.

## Existing records

- No stored record changes, and no migration is necessary.
- Attempts recorded before 0.73.0 have no `observed_at`, and their waits count as elapsed. They keep their
  classification, and the answer recovery does not apply to them.
- Assignments recorded before 0.73.0 have `unit_allowance_floor` `null`. Posts recorded before 0.73.0 read as `gate`
  `passed`.
- The record fingerprints (`ci/record_fingerprint.py`) of all 21 live research records are the same under 0.72.4
  and 0.73.0: every assignment state, effective attempt, refusal code, attempt authentication and rebuilt request
  digest.
- A record moves to 0.73.0 after `record-check` with 0.73.0 passes on it (Operations, below).

| `review_provider_refused` | stop | A second API error that names safeguards. |

| `review_turn_failures_exhausted` | stop | 8 failed attempts at one turn position. |
| `review_parts_budget_exhausted` | stop | A parts v2 read at the floor overflowed the output cap, or a continuing read would leave originals unread. |

New record kind: `review_parts_restart`. New fields: `observed_at`, `provider_diagnostic`, `answer_recovery`,
`unit_allowance_floor`, `after_post`, and the post's `gate`.

## What was measured and what was not

- Record fingerprints on copies of the 21 live records, under 0.72.4 and under the code of the readers of 0.73.0:
  no difference on any record. The fingerprint of the bunkbed copy took 866 s under 0.72.4 and 412 s under 0.73.0,
  and that of the graph rigidity copy 173 s and 125 s.
- `record-check` on 12 record copies during development: 9 passed all six steps; 3 (extremal, sidorenko,
  stretched-lr) keep one historical request each that 0.72.4 does not rebuild either. On fresh copies of both
  bunkbed records with `problem-post-external` recorded, all 23 chain heads of each plan `done`.
- The first runs of `record-check` on the live records found that its sandbox profile denied writes to
  `/dev/null`. The Codex runtime read failed inside the check, the state memo was then off, and `status` on the kmp
  copy took 370.6 s instead of 45.3 s. With `/dev/null` exempted it takes 47.4 s inside the sandbox. This release
  contains the fix.
- `record-check` with the final code on the 21 live records: 11 of them had passed all six steps when the pull request opened, and none had failed. The checks that had not finished
  when the pull request opened run after it; their results are in the record of this work.
- `status`, `next` and `obligations` under 0.72.4 and 0.73.0 on the same copies: on the bunkbed copy, 0.73.0 took 259, 258 and 256 s, and 0.72.4 took 322, 333 and 334 s; on the
  2409.17337 copy, 0.73.0 took 344, 335 and 336 s, and 0.72.4 took 327, 333 and 325 s. Both are within the
  limit of 1.25 times the time of 0.72.4 plus 10 s. The other copies were measured after the pull request
  opened, under a heavily loaded machine.

Not measured: a run of `review-series` with real model calls on a record copy, and the quality of a parts series
against a single-input review (open since 0.72.0).

## Known limits and open items

- `status` takes about 5 minutes on the bunkbed record under load. About 170 s of it is JSON serialization inside the
  pinned transports while recorded attempts are authenticated. 0.74.0 designs an authentication path that rebuilds
  only the requests that decide the current report.
- Three live records keep one historical request each that no release rebuilds: `attempt-result-astra-sharp-three-v2`
  (extremal), `assignment-adjudication-post-activity-run` (sidorenko) and `probe-astra` (stretched-lr). 0.72.4 fails
  on the same requests. The record check reports them, and they do not block the release.
- The openness chain of the bunkbed record cannot finish on its route: its mandatory final input has 1,303,063
  characters, over the limit of 1,300,000. The problem was posted on exactory.ai without `gate post`. Its session
  records that post with `problem-post-external` after the update.
- A running session keeps the hooks that it loaded. After an update, start a new session. A hook launcher that falls
  back to a newer installed version is a proposal to the owner, and it is not in this release.
- Deferred to 0.74.0: hooks that read shell text (v11 H15), abstracts of DOI-only aliases, v7 R9 to R12, and smaller
  partial fixes.

## Operations

- Before a record continues under 0.73.0, run `record-check` with 0.73.0 on it. When the report says `ready` true,
  update the record's wrapper or command, make sure that it runs 0.73.0, and start a new session.
- The release procedure for the live records is in `misc/harness-improvement/LIVE-RECORDS.md` of the exactory
  repository: the record fingerprint under the previous release and the candidate, `record-check` with the candidate,
  and a time comparison of `status`, `next` and `obligations`.
