# Exactory 0.71.0

This release carries the records that were still open after 0.70.0. An oversized problem result returns to the part
delivery of 0.69.x unless a staged review is requested; every ordinary review is judged on what its exact request
delivered; a request is checked before any reviewer call exists, and every ordinary call is captured so that a later
process can finish it; new ordinary assignments store their history by reference; evaluations bound what they
retain; enumerations, batches and the citation check state their bounds and coverage; exports, packages and deposits
stream; provider pacing is shared across local processes; a resumable job captures and extracts large originals; and
the math-solver audits, replay, render and hooks stop repeating work. The release follows 0.70.2. The work is
recorded in `misc/harness-improvement/20261101173008-release-0-71-0-remaining-records` of the exactory repository.

## Why

On 2026-11-01 the owner asked that the nine records named after 0.70.0 be sorted: what 0.70.0 shipped goes to
`[DONE]/`, what needs no further change goes to `[NO_NEED]/`, and the rest is fixed in one release. Six records were
closed as shipped in 0.69.0 to 0.69.20 and none was judged unnecessary. Three stayed open: the part-delivery record
(step 2 of section 19.12 of its design, the storage and command foundations, ships here; steps 3 to 6 go to 0.72.0),
the scalability audit (efforts E2 to E6 ship here; the E1 findings go to 0.72.0 with the staged review v3 work) and
the citations record (its documentation correction ships here).

The owner's second decision fixes a defect found in the code of 0.70.0, not reproduced on a record:
`review_problem_parts.prepare_packet` sent every new prospective result that did not fit one input into staged v2
whenever the scale preflight passed, and every staged slot listed `review_quality_measurement_pending` with no way
to lift it, so `gate post` was never ready for such a record, while 0.69.20 posted the same record through the part
delivery. The D1 design reproduced it at the unit level on Python 3.9.6 and 3.12.8: an ordinary request of
1,440,640 characters against the route limit of 1,300,000 entered staged v2; with automatic entry off, the first v1
part request was 721,728 characters. Six GPT 6 Astra designs (D1 to D6) checked every audit finding against
`a89c571` with read-only probes; the measurements quoted below are those probes, taken on 0.70.0, not measurements
of this release.

## What changed

### Review delivery and entry

- Entry (D1-1, owner decision 2). `prepare_packet` keeps a complete ordinary request when it fits and carries every
  evidence body, starts a v1 part series otherwise, and continues a recorded v1 series as before; staged v2 starts
  only through an explicit `staged_upgrade`, and `allows_staged_entry` is removed. A new oversized focused
  adjudication keeps its ordinary packet. A new series, also the refreshed slot after a v1 series, whose ordinary
  request fits and carries every body is delivered whole, as 0.70.x did on local routes; the 0.69.x rule that skips
  the ordinary request after a part turn is not restored. Commit `676b750` runs the three 0.70.2 part-delivery tests
  under these records and this rule: two read histories through `read_history`, and the earlier-series
  objection-body test makes its ordinary request withhold a body so it still exercises the v1 planner.
- Openness final input (D1-2, F08). `preflight_final_input` measures a lower bound of the mandatory final input of a
  reading chain in the route's own representation before the first turn and before each later turn, and refuses
  with `review_input_too_large` (phase `final_preflight`); where no sound lower bound exists it reports unknown. The
  design's probe fitted a first turn of 122,834 characters and failed the final at 1,594,239 against 1,300,000.
- Delivered evidence (D2-1, F04, L2-02, H03). `review_delivered_evidence.read_attempt_delivery` authenticates one
  named completed attempt and projects what its request actually delivered in three views: the current input, the
  citation history that the recorded protocol permits, and the coverage witnesses. No saved packet restores a body.
- Ordinary citations and direction coverage (D2-2, D2-3, F04, H03, L2-02). Contract validation, the recorded-review
  audit, bar delivery checks, problem coverage, focused adjudication validation, `supplied_context` and
  `assess_direction_reviews` use the delivery of the exact authenticated attempt instead of the saved packets of the
  frozen lineage; the rules of 0.69.4, 0.69.19 and the staged task graph are kept. A delivery that cannot be
  authenticated or decoded credits nothing and is reported through the existing codes, with no fallback to a saved
  packet; a refresh of such a review stays permitted.
- Source reply batches (D2-4, F12). `bar_sources.continuation` budgeted only new full text, so inventory pages and
  abstracts could exceed the reviewer input. `review_source_batches.plan_packet` measures the complete serialized
  request for every reply kind and includes whole replies in request order while they fit; a deferred reply becomes
  `deferred_capacity` and stays pending, and passage allowances are charged only for included replies. An
  indivisible reply that cannot fit refuses with `review_input_too_large`, a complete-visible closure that does not
  fit refuses before any invocation, and an unchanged inherited unavailable request creates no new call
  (`review_source_no_progress`). The choice is frozen in the packet under `source_reply_batch`.
- Openness v4 (D2-5, F12 defect 3). New turns of problem chains use `exactory-openness-reading-v4`: v3's shape plus
  the rule that a final turn's reading response must request at least one passage that the harness delivers within
  the remaining allowance, or it is refused with `invalid_reading_response`; under v3 such a response continued.

### Review calls, capture and preparation

- Dispatch preflight (D1-3, H11, H05; supervisor decision 7). `review_preflight` measures the exact request in the
  route's own representation and, for a native reading chain, the mandatory final-input floor; a run, a route probe
  and an output correction check it before an invocation exists and refuse with `review_input_too_large` (phase
  `dispatch_preflight`), where 0.70.0 recorded the invocation first. The report marks each field as measured or
  unknown and is kept in the new invocation and attempt records. An initial problem result whose run is refused
  this way frees its slot; one that never ran and fits keeps it.
- Call allowance (D3-6, L2-12; replaces D1-4 and D1-5). `review-budget` records the opt-in `review_budget/store`
  with `max_calls` and a reason. With it, the start transaction of every ordinary call, probe, correction and
  staged reservation counts the admitted calls from the authoritative records and refuses with
  `review_budget_exhausted` when none remains; each admission counts once, and failed and unknown outcomes stay
  counted. `review_budget_below_charged` refuses an allowance below the calls already admitted;
  `intermediate/reviews/usage.json` shows the counts.
- Scoped ordinary preparation (D4-3, C02, H07). An ordinary preparation binds allowlisted groups of records instead
  of the whole revision, so an unrelated commit leaves it valid and a change to a bound group invalidates it with
  `review_task_dependency_changed`. `Store.read_records` returns requested rows at one revision without replaying
  history; it is advisory and grants no readiness, authentication, launch or capacity release.
- Call capture (D4-4, H08). After the transport returns, the live call authority commits one immutable
  `review_call_observation` that binds the call's operation, invocation, request, process and exact observation
  (for a correction on `codex_cli_v1`, with the native rollout proof). Repeating the original command with the same
  request id returns an existing receipt first and otherwise finishes from the capture without calling the
  transport. A new run of an assignment whose invocation is captured but unfinished is refused with
  `review_invocation_pending`, and since `5cdd787` the message names the original command with its invocation id
  and request id; reconciliation releases capacity only.
- History references (D4-5, C04, ordinary part). New ordinary assignments store `history_ref` instead of the inline
  history that every continuation repeated: the message contents go to the artifact store, immutable pages of at
  most 32 messages hold the ordered metadata with each message's source and occurrence and share unchanged prefixes
  between turns, and the root binds the message count, the pages and the digest of the logical history.
  `read_history` serves every consumer and refuses a record with both fields (`review_history_ambiguous`) or a page
  or body that does not match (`review_history_invalid`). Transports receive the identical materialized history.

### Literature, cohorts, batches and exports

- Population queries (D3-1, D3-10, L2-07). `population_query` cut its ranked matches at the limit without saying so.
  The result states the total, returned, omitted and remaining counts and gives `next_cursor` while matches remain;
  `population-query --cursor` continues; a cursor from another query fails with `invalid_input`, one from an older
  revision with `stale_cursor`, each naming the command that restarts the query.
- Abstract batches (D3-2, D3-10, L2-08). A batch was bounded only by its item count. A byte bound applies to the
  complete encoded file (`--max-input-bytes`, default 262,144), `--max-output-tokens` with
  `--estimated-output-tokens-per-item` add an item ceiling that the manifest labels as estimated, each file is
  published as soon as it is full, and `manifest.json` is written incomplete first and marked complete only after
  the whole enumeration. `batch_item_too_large` and `batch_output_budget_too_small` name a runnable command.
  Members without an abstract count as missing.
- Verification depth (D3-3, L2-06). A verification kept ten hits per query, twenty search readings and ten core full
  texts. A verification depth plan (`profile: verification`, a reason, a nonempty `stopping_criterion`) sets
  `hits_per_query`, `search_readings` and `core_fulltexts` to values at least the defaults; the selection is kept
  per profile, and the selection that earlier releases wrote as `current` is still read as the research plan.
- Citation report v2 (D3-4, L2-11). Version 2 separates `lookup_complete` from `all_verified`; `no_query`,
  `network_error` and the new `search_incomplete` stop the lookup and the gate, and `year_mismatch` stays a
  warning. Searches page through the provider request builders and report their coverage; a negative conclusion
  needs complete traversal, `--search-pages N` (default one page of five results) raises the bound, and a published
  version replaces a preprint only after a complete search with exactly one match and equal author lists. Submit
  and deposit still report citations without blocking.
- Study packets (D3-5, L2-04). `_source_closure` and `_branch_inputs` group readings, bundles, admissions and
  checkpoints once per call instead of once per selected version or cycle; packets keep their bytes.
- Streamed exports, packages and deposits (D3-8, D4-7, D3-9, L2-13, C13). Deposit packages, native exports,
  reviewer deliveries and uploads no longer hold every payload in memory: the archive queue holds references and
  members are written from verified private copies; exports and deliveries are staged privately and renamed into
  place with the manifest last, so a failure exposes no partial delivery; and `continue_deposit` freezes and
  verifies every file it will still send before its first remote write and sends each as a seekable stream with
  `Content-Length`.

### Full text and acquisition

- HTTP bodies (D6-1, C14). `HttpClient.get` copied the whole growing body after every chunk (2,155,872,256 bytes for
  a 16 MiB body); one buffer per attempt is now frozen once, and captured bytes, status and errors are unchanged.
- Provider pacing (D6-2, C15). `provider_pacing` keeps the current scheduling state in one SQLite file per user
  (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/exactory`, or `~/.local/state/exactory`). Every attempt is admitted before DNS; a `Retry-After`
  cooldown is recorded when the headers arrive and blocks the whole provider group; the arXiv hosts form one group
  with the 3-second interval and one active connection, held through a lock file until the response closes. Full
  state refuses with `provider_state_capacity`, corrupt or busy state with `provider_state_unavailable` before any
  network work, and a wait beyond the call's allowance with `rate_limited`.
- Imports (D6-3, D6-8, C16). `parse_mapped` decodes a mapped JSON response once, coverage is collected in one pass,
  and `import-response` checks a committed request first and replays it without reading the response; a new
  response or payload file above 8 MiB is refused with `import_input_too_large` before parsing.
- Transfers and extraction (D6-5, D6-6, H10, L2-09). `HttpClient.get_stream` streams each attempt's body into an
  artifact under byte, retained-byte and time allowances. `fulltext_transfer` continues with byte ranges only while
  the final URL, a strong ETag, identity encoding and the total length stay the same (`fulltext_transfer_changed`
  otherwise), streams one complete response where safe ranges are unavailable (`fulltext_resume_unsupported`), and
  records a verified assembled original as `capture_method: http_range_v1`. `extraction_units.ExtractionEngine`
  extracts a PDF page by page under `pdf-pages-v1` and commits each completed page before the next, so a later
  process resumes after the last committed page; a page beyond its allowance stays pending with its index, usage,
  limit and recovery action.
- Full-text jobs (D6-7, D6-8). `fulltext-start`, `fulltext-resume` and the read-only `fulltext-status` run a durable
  job whose transfer units, network attempts and extraction pages are `fulltext_unit` records and which publishes a
  `source_assembly` only when complete; its allowances can only rise, with a reason. Acquisition commands hold a
  per-target lock (`acquisition_in_progress`); a partition at the minimum width that reaches the provider's query
  ceiling keeps a permanent `query_ceiling` diagnosis, and `resume` with `recheck` tries it again.

### Store and evaluation

- Stream primitives (D3-7, D6-4, D4-7). `ArtifactStore.put_stream`, `put_file`, `verify`, `open_verified` and
  `materialize_verified`, with `workspace.open_file` and `file_streams.freeze_file`, copy and verify in 1 MiB chunks
  and publish only after the complete content is verified; check-mode stores refuse stream publication.
- Retention and runtime scope (D4-1, C03, C04, H06, H07). Optional retained values share one weighted LRU per root
  evaluation, 64 MiB by default, where an evaluation kept them all for the life of the command; a budget of 0 keeps
  nothing, and the exporters use it. `runtime_scope` observes the reviewer runtime at the start and the end of one
  read or preparation instead of once per cached state, and a change in between refuses with
  `review_runtime_changed`. `visit_lineage` validates each lineage node once per evaluation. No cache outlives its
  command.
- Coverage reuse (D4-2, C05). Bundles are indexed once per snapshot instead of scanned per original,
  `current_readings` and `fulltext_coverage` are reused within the command's evaluation by exact records view,
  version, target and eligible-reading set, and `assess_post` shares its attempt and openness assessments; reports
  and obligations are unchanged.

### Math-solver hooks and views

- Evidence audits and replay (D5-1, D5-2, C06, C08). An audit context created per top-level audit keeps successful
  manifest validations by digest, verify and fresh, so a layered graph of 25 manifests needs 25 validations instead
  of 8,191. `replay` reduces into one state it owns instead of deep-copying the accumulated state before every
  event; the public `apply_event` keeps its contract, and the canonical state of the history fixtures is unchanged.
- Acknowledgments and refresh (D5-3, C09). `after_legacy` records a native or journal acknowledgment alone and
  observes the producing node in a separate event; if that observation fails, the acknowledgment stays recorded and
  `recovery_required` names the nodes to refresh, where 0.70.0 discarded it. `search refresh --spec FILE` with
  `{"node_ids": [...]}` observes 1 to 256 admitted nodes in one event, and Stop stops at 255 changed nodes with
  `recovery_required`, their ids and `has_more: true`.
- Rendering (D5-4, C10). The renderer indexes checkpoints, assessments and joins once before the node loop and
  hashes listed files in 1 MiB reads; a test compares 25 documents and 48 JSON records with the previous renderer.
- Hooks (D5-5, C11, H15). The read-only `search guard-status --json` returns `{protocol:
  exactory-search-guard-status-v1, objective_id, contract_digest, revision}` after a full replay and nothing else;
  the math guard checks every target of a patch and queries each root once under one 20-second deadline, and an
  unexpected failure of its own checks now denies the call. Views are published under the render lock after the
  command's last commit and outside the writer lock, an old snapshot never overwrites a newer render, and internal
  hook-stop defers all advisory rendering; `search render` repairs a stale view.
- Attack activity (D4-9, C12). When the log is trimmed to its 200 most recent entries, the latest harness-command
  entry of each identified session stays, so a busy attack keeps recording the session's ordinary shell commands;
  an exclusive `fcntl` lock on the attack directory covers read, append and rewrite, and a temporary file replaces
  the log atomically.

## Known limits

- 0.72.0 carries the E1 findings of the audit (F01, F02, F05, F06, F07, F09, F11, L2-01, L2-03, L2-05, H01, H02,
  H04) with staged review v3, steps 3 to 6 of section 19.12 of the part-delivery design; D4-6 (the bounded
  frontier, narrow polling and sibling dispatch) and the staged v2 epoch normalization of D4-5, which after decision
  2 serve only an explicit `staged_upgrade`, whose slot cannot post before the quality measurement (decision 5);
  D4-8 (incremental output groups and series indexes), which the design builds on D4-6 (decision 10); the
  whole-operation deadline D1-6 (decision 6); the
  quality measurement, A then C, after v3 (decision 3); and L2-10, H09 and F10 (the scientific delivery ceilings,
  the aggregate export and the native review context growth) with the staged replacement of an oversized
  complete-visible delivery, each needing v3 steps 3 to 5. Every staged slot still lists
  `review_quality_measurement_pending`; nothing in this release qualifies staged v2 or the part delivery.
- Output-token and duration estimates are reported as unknown: the lane built them from every earlier invocation of
  the route, which rescanned and re-authenticated the route's whole history on each call and stored the list of
  observations in every new record; the supervisor removed them, and `review_work_estimate_exceeds_route` is not
  added, because no relationship between such estimates and completion has been measured.
- Full-history validation is unchanged: every authoritative snapshot and every commit validates the whole history,
  measured by the D4 design on 0.70.0 at 2.307 s and 2.027 s for a copy of the unknotting record and 1.997 s and
  1.715 s for a copy of the KMP record. Every full-text job step (`read_job`, `JobArtifacts.check_space`, the
  extraction runner) takes an authoritative scoped snapshot. Existing inline histories are not rewritten, and exact
  recorded requests still repeat delivered history.
- A crash before a capture commits still loses the result. The retention budget bounds optional retained values,
  not process memory; the snapshot, the readiness reports, metadata and one parsed JSON value at a time stay in
  memory. The batch bounds label the output capacity as an estimate, or unknown, and do not establish that a
  coordinator's prompt fits a model route.
- The BibTeX directive forms that 0.69.16 does not cover (a newline before the delimiter, a directive that does not
  start a line, a parenthesized directive) stay known limits: their text is still read as evidence.
- Provider pacing coordinates the processes of one machine and one user, not several machines. The JATS extraction
  keeps its bounds, the legacy `fulltext` command and `HttpClient.get` keep the 128 MiB bound, and a transfer whose
  server offers no strong validator cannot resume.
- No end-to-end latency or memory improvement of this release was measured. Two cleanups wait for 0.72.0: the part
  series delivery decoding that `research_decisions` repeats, and the `staged_entry` plumbing kept for the
  compatibility test of the frozen 0.70.0 module.

## Operations

GPT 6 Astra at effort xhigh wrote six designs in read-only lanes (D1 staged entry and checks before work, D2
delivered evidence, D3 silent truncation and study pipelines, D4 storage and evaluation reuse, D5 math-solver audits
and controller state, D6 acquisition and extraction); the model and effort of every lane were verified in the
